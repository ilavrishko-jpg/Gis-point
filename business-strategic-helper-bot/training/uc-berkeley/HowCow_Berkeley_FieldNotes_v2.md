## Part 4   Field Notes and Key Learnings
A structured summary of insights from sessions, workshops, and conversations across the full program. Written to be used — not filed.

#### How US Venture Capital Actually Works
The VC model runs on math that most founders underestimate. In any portfolio: 50% of bets are lost, 35% break even, 10% return 2x, and only 5% make the fund. You need to be that 5% — not a safe bet, a massive one.
Funding stages map to revenue, not time. Pre-Seed is under $10M raised with zero revenue. Seed is under $4M with roughly $500K in revenue. Series A is under $15M with around $3M revenue. Series B is under $40M with $6M revenue. VCs expect a return cycle of 20-22 months.
US investors have one question: how fast and how big? Survival thinking does not land in the US. Growth orientation does. Show a global mindset from day one, big ambitions, and a fast path — not a roadmap, a sprint.
Fundraising is always happening. It is a shared role between co-founders. One person stays on product, one on business, one on relationships. Show this division clearly in the deck. Show the US entity. It removes a major blocker immediately.
VCs give money to people they already know or who come through someone they trust. Networking is not optional — it is the mechanism. Accelerators give three things: narrative, connections, and investors.
When meeting investors: lead with signal-driven evidence (signed pilots, LOIs, recommendations). Show the delta between problem and solution. Be adaptive. Spend funds on growth, not longevity — deploy when you have capital.

#### How to Build a Pitch That Works
There are two elements to every pitch: what you are pitching (content) and how you pitch it (delivery). Both matter equally. Neglecting either kills the meeting.
The NABC framework from SRI is the most reliable structure for innovation pitching. Need always comes first — what is the big market need and who exactly is the customer. Then Approach — why you will succeed, what makes your path different. Then Benefits — what the customer actually gets, not features but outcomes. Then Competition — what alternatives exist and why yours wins. Hook at the start. Close at the end. Ask for exactly what you want.
Three pitch formats serve different moments. The 30-60 second intro is for hallways and networking — catch attention, one sentence: what, for whom, what result. The 5-10 minute pitch event version makes them want a follow-up — NABC structure, one number, clear ask. The 30-45 minute investor meeting shows the full picture with Q&A — know who is in the room before you walk in.
Know who your biggest supporter in the room is. Pitch to them first. You are responsible for your own outcome — no one else. Understand your customer completely before you try to sell anything.
The most important metric to show: the delta between the problem and your solution. Make it measurable. Make it unavoidable.

#### Evidence Is Everything — How to Build It Right
Without evidence, nothing else matters. Not the deck, not the story, not the team slide. A good experimental result survives the next pivot and the next market cycle.
The shift in language matters as much as the data itself. Not 'we are the best on 30% of farms.' Instead: 'farms with HowCow reduced losses by 80% compared to farms without us.' The question every investor and customer will ask is: compared to what?
There are five valid comparison frameworks. First: same treatment conditions without HowCow versus farms with HowCow. Second: before versus after on the same farm. Third: similar farms without HowCow versus farms with HowCow. Fourth: different farm sizes — does it scale? Fifth: control group without intervention versus with HowCow.
Minimum viable evidence: 5-6 farms to show a pattern. 20 units for statistical weight. Time: 1-2 lactation cycles or one season (3-4 months minimum). Design the experiment now so the result will still be meaningful after the next product update.

#### AI-Powered Marketing and Visibility
Marketing is more important than product at this stage. There are zero second chances with the same investor. The first impression is the only impression.
The discovery model has changed. People now begin their research through AI answer engines — ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI — before they visit a website. Being found is no longer only about SEO. It is about being present in the places AI systems use to generate answers. LinkedIn company pages are among the most cited sources in AI-generated search results.
Consistency matters more than audience size. Regular publishing with clear expertise and a real point of view is what gets cited. Generic AI content is worse than silence — it creates noise without credibility.
Build a content system around three tracks. Track A is the persona portfolio: three profiles — the CEO voice, the potential investor voice, the farm owner voice. Different content for different audiences. Track B is market monitoring: weekly scan of regulations, research, competitor news, Bird Flu signals. Original opinions, not reshared news. Track C is the content specialist: a style guide for all posts, a prompt that generates content in your tone for each channel. Post 2-3 times per week.
Use synthetic personas to stress-test the deck before a real investor sees it. Build a structured AI prompt to review the pitch deck across 7-8 dimensions: relevance, evidence, market size, solution clarity, risks, buyer questions, what is missing. Run it before every major meeting.
The most timely story right now: HowCow Bird Flu early detection results. Post it. Put it in the deck. It is the sharpest proof point available.

#### AI in Organizations — What Actually Matters
AI does not fix broken processes. It amplifies them — the good and the bad equally. If a workflow is broken, AI makes it fail faster and more visibly.
The most dangerous failure mode: resistance does not come from leadership and it does not come from frontline workers. It comes from the middle of the organization — legal, HR, risk and compliance — the people who are neither deciding nor building.
We do not sell AI. We sell results. The right question for HowCow is not how much we reduce costs for the farmer. It is how much additional revenue the farmer earns because of us.
The strongest AI-native org structure for a startup at this stage: everyone builds (IC builder/operator), someone checks customer outcomes directly (DRI), and someone stays ahead of the AI strategy (AI founder). No middle management. Token-maxing over headcount.
Embed AI in the flow of work. If people have to switch context to use a tool, they will not use it. The Toggle Tax is real.
Keep generating S curves. Start the next wave before the previous one finishes. The delta between where you are and where the next wave begins is where new market opportunities live.

#### Carbon Credits, Provenance Milk, and New Revenue Tracks
The collar is already a dMRV device. Behavioral data maps to a methane proxy. A verified methane proxy maps to carbon credits. The infrastructure to generate $400-450 per cow per year in additional revenue exists — it just needs the right packaging and a verified methodology.
A2 Milk is the cleanest premium opportunity. A2 milk sells at 2x the price in the US, Australia, and China. The market problem is that there is no reliable verification that milk actually comes from A2/A2 cows. HowCow already knows every cow. Tie genotype to collar ID, issue blockchain-verified A2 certificates per batch. This is not new technology — it is new packaging of existing data.
Terroir Milk is the longer-term provenance play. Think wine provenance applied to dairy. GPS, breed, diet, and season tied to a verified fat-protein fingerprint. Every batch unique. Premium positioning built on traceability HowCow already generates.

#### Data Strategy and Machine Learning Foundations
FAIR data is the foundation: Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable. Every data point needs metrics and attributes. Raw data without context is not an asset — it is noise.
JSON to Markdown is the right format direction: human-first, agent-friendly. Structured text that both a person and an AI agent can parse without transformation.
The CRISP-DM process is the standard ML workflow: Business Understanding, Data Understanding, Data Preparation, Modeling, Evaluation, Deployment, back to Data Understanding. It is a loop, not a pipeline.
In Dataiku, the HowCow build priority is: (1) raw collar data as Dataset 1 — temperature, activity, rumination; (2) Prepare Recipe to calculate deviation from each cow's individual baseline; (3) anomaly score per cow per day as Dataset 2; (4) classification model — anomaly score above threshold triggers an alert; (5) daily batch scoring recipe — new data in, forecast out; (6) output dataset with risk scores for the CRM app. Target AUC above 0.85. Dataiku trial closes June 2, 2026.
The right question is always: compared to what? This applies to data, to models, and to investor conversations. Establish the baseline first. The model is only as credible as the comparison it is built against.

#### California Agriculture — Market Context
California is the number one agricultural state in the US with $61.2 billion in total production and $23.8 billion in exports. Approximately 7.5 million acres of irrigated cropland. 1.5 million acres of almond production — 80% of world supply. 420,000 acres of walnuts.
The decade from 2010 to 2019 was historically profitable for both crops. Since 2020, over-supply, Covid logistics disruption, rising costs, tariffs, and geopolitical conflicts have created significant sustained market pressure. The majority of both crops is exported — making California farmers highly sensitive to trade policy shifts.
Water is 30% of total farm operating costs in California. Healthier cows use water more efficiently. California has specific water banking, pump, and delivery policies that affect any farm-based technology deployment.
For HowCow's California strategy: the ROI conversation is the only entry point that works. Farmers are under simultaneous pressure from multiple directions. Reducing preventable losses — $444 per mastitis case, $300 per missed heat cycle — is an immediate and concrete value proposition in this environment.

#### Negotiation — Core Principles
Negotiation is communication. Not combat, not performance — communication.
There are two core tensions in every negotiation. The first is between creating value and distributing it — cooperation and competition running at the same time. The second is between empathy and assertiveness — understanding their position while holding your own.
Never mistake someone's stated position for their actual interest. Find out what they actually need. The position is what they say. The interest is why they say it. The deal gets done at the level of interests, not positions.
You can cooperate and compete simultaneously. That is not a contradiction — it is the craft.
