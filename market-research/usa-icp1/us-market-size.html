The number that reframes everything

US Surveying & Mapping Services: 17,511 businesses, 68,810 employees (IBISWorld 2025). That is an average of 3.9 people per business.

The industry is overwhelmingly sole practitioners and micro-firms. A 30–200 person surveying firm is a statistical outlier — roughly 3 in every 100 employer firms in the industry. Your Tier 1 is not a slice of a big market; it is the thin top of a very steep pyramid.

The US regulatory driver is stronger than the EU one

The RDC profile rests on EU Directive 2019/1936 — a network-wide safety assessment at least every five years. The US equivalent, 23 CFR Part 490 + HPMS, bites considerably harder.

Annual full-extent reporting on the Interstate is federally mandated, non-optional, recurring volume — the same force the ICP is built on, running on a tighter clock.

But the US pool is tiny and already consolidated

Network-level DOT work is served by a handful of specialists — Pathway Services (reported to serve more than half of US state DOTs), Fugro Roadware, Mandli Communications, Dynatest and WayLink, with IMS, Roadway Asset Services, ICC-IMS and Transmap behind them. Below that sits a longer tail doing county and municipal pavement management.

Two consequences. It is a named-account market, not a segment — 35–70 firms in the ICP band means you can enumerate the entire US pool in a week, so research depth per account beats list volume. And the say-no test removes the biggest names: Fugro, Stantec, WSP, Michael Baker and ARA are all far above 250 people, and several run exactly the "30+ person in-house processing centre" the profile calls out as competitor rather than customer.

4 · AEC / scan-to-BIM

NAICS 541330 · 541310 Not sized

142,387 engineering enterprises and 67,785 architectural practices in the US (IBISWorld). Both codes are dominated by MEP, structural and process work — all explicitly anti-ICP. Only the thin minority running a scan-to-BIM or point-cloud production line qualifies, and we have no basis to set that fit rate. Deliberately left unsized rather than guessed: at this scale a wrong percentage produces a TAM that is confidently wrong by tens of thousands.

Volume and saturation are different questions. By volume it's Texas → Florida → California. By LQ it's Alaska (2.38) → Florida (1.92) → Texas (1.85) → Louisiana (1.81). Texas and Florida win on both at once, which is what makes them the obvious first wave. These seven are the states named in the BLS release. The other 44 are not ranked here — that needs the Census run. What the arithmetic does say: after the top three take 36%, the remaining 48 states split the rest at roughly 680 employees each, so small states (RI, VT, DE, ND, SD, WY) almost certainly hold zero or one ICP1 firm apiece.

Three things worth arguing about

RDC v1.0 puts the USA outside Tier 1 — and this is US research

The Road Data Contractors profile tiers its geography as Tier 1 = Poland, DACH, CEE, UK/Ireland, Benelux, Nordics; Tier 2 = France, Iberia, Italy, Western Balkans; Tier 3 = Gulf, ANZ, Canada. The three US firms it names — Pathway Services, Roadway Asset Services, ICC-IMS — sit under "Tier 3, opportunistic".

So the newest ICP deliberately deprioritises the market this report covers. That is a defensible call: the anchor account is Polish, the statutory cycles are European, and the US pool is small and consolidated. But it needs settling before it drives spend — either the US moves up a tier for RDC on the strength of the 23 CFR 490 annual cycle, or RDC is a Europe-first play and the US work stays on segments 1 and 3.

Telecom has a route-to-market problem

Broadband operators usually buy network documentation through their engineering or OSP contractor, not direct. The contractor may be the real account and the operator only the end client. Qualify who holds the budget before working the operator — otherwise you sell the whole way through and lose to the incumbent contractor at the last step.

Data sovereignty bites hardest here

Critical-infrastructure network records carry restrictions that can forbid offshore processing outright. It is already an anti-ICP gate, and it disqualifies more accounts in telecom than anywhere else in ICP1. Test it in the first call — it costs one question and it is expensive to discover after a proposal.

What the size implies for GTM

  • Roughly 600–950 target companies nationally. Segment 1 plus segment 3; segment 2 sits inside segment 1. At that scale the entire US ICP1 is a nameable list — you can build it once and work it, rather than running volume outbound at a market that isn't big enough to absorb it.
  • The 10–30 band is your upgrade pipeline, not noise. ~1,250 firms sit directly below the Tier 1 floor. Tier 2 today, Tier 1 in two years, and far cheaper to hold a relationship with now than to win cold later.
  • RDC is 35–70 accounts, so treat it as a list, not a funnel. The whole US pool fits on one page. That inverts the economics: a week of research per account is proportionate, and the paid-pilot offer in the ICP — 50–100 km on their own spec, reported as hours/km, QC first-pass rate and cost/km — is the right instrument for a market this small. Volume outbound would exhaust it in a month.
  • Texas and Florida alone are 29% of the market. BLS QCEW 2023 industry employment in NAICS 541370: Texas 9,139 (16.6%, LQ 1.85), Florida 6,684 (12.1%, LQ 1.92), California 4,085 (7.4%) — top three together 36% of national employment. Note this reverses the ranking you'd get from counting licensed surveyors: Florida is a bigger industry market than California even though California has more licensed individuals. A territory plan that treats all 50 states equally wastes most of its effort. For RDC specifically the unit is the state DOT, not state population — all 52 procure on the same federal clock regardless of size.
  • Telecom roughly doubles the ICP1 count but on a different motion, a different buyer and a harder sovereignty gate. Treat it as a second beachhead to test, not as a bolt-on to the existing surveying play.

Method & honesty notes

Measured figures are lifted from published sources, named in each table. Derived figures are modelled: national employment and business counts are hard constraints, and the size distribution is fitted to them assuming the Pareto-shaped firm distribution typical of US professional services. Bucket boundaries that cut a band are split log-uniformly. Ranges are given because the result is genuinely sensitive to that fit — treat them as order-of-magnitude, not precision.

What is still blocked. The exact 51-state × size-band cross-tab needs US Census County Business Patterns, and api.census.gov is blocked by this environment's network policy. fetch_cbp.py in this repo produces that table in about 30 seconds from any machine with open outbound HTTPS, and its output supersedes every derived figure here.

Establishments vs. companies. Census counts physical locations; IBISWorld counts businesses. A multi-office firm counts once here but would count per-office in the Census run — good for territory planning, misleading for tier assignment, since a 30-person office of a 400-person firm has a corporate bench and fails the "no bench" gate.
