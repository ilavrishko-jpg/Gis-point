# Landscape of startup studios (venture studios / venture builders) launching into North America — state as of 2026

> Method note: research was done on 2026-11-19 through web search only. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (hosts such as startupstudios.com, metz.substack.com, signatureblock.co, en.wikipedia.org, uea.ac.uk were not reachable), so most facts below come from search-result extracts of the cited pages, not from a full read of each page. Figures marked "secondary" or "directory" were not checked against the primary report. Dates of each data point are given because many studio statistics are several years old.

## 1. Who are the leading studios in North America and what are their track records?

### Takeaway
The North American top tier is a small group of well-capitalised studios with dedicated funds: Flagship Pioneering (biotech, $14B AUM, 100+ companies), Atomic ($320M Fund IV, ~$750M AUM), High Alpha (B2B SaaS, $260M+ raised, 30+ to 50+ companies), AI Fund ($190M Fund II), Science Inc, Expa (~$200M fund), PSL, Betaworks, Redesign Health and Diagram (Canada). Company-count and exit figures are mostly self-reported and vary by source and date.

**Idealab**
- Founded 1996 in Pasadena by Bill Gross; described as the longest-running technology incubator; created over 150 companies with more than 45 IPOs and acquisitions. — [startupintros.com profile](https://startupintros.com/orgs/idealab-operating-companies); [Diamandis blog](https://www.diamandis.com/blog/9-key-lessons-derived-from-150-startups)

### Inferences
- The best-funded North American studios now look like funds with an in-house build team (Atomic, High Alpha, PSL, Expa, Science all pair a studio with a fee-and-carry fund); pure "holding company" studios are rare at the top.
- Several first-generation studios moved away from pure company building: Betaworks to cohort camps plus seed investing, Human Ventures to a fund, Idealab's major outcomes are 1990s–2000s.
- Healthcare studio building slowed after 2022 (Redesign's two layoff rounds), while AI-focused studio capital grew in 2025 (AI Fund II, Betaworks III, Antler's US allocation).
- Company counts are not comparable across studios: some count spinouts, some count "built and invested", and dates range from 2021 to 2026.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) company count, fund size or exit list was found for PSL, Science Inc, Expa, Redesign Health or AI Fund; the figures above are 2018–2023 unless dated otherwise.
- FutureSight: nothing reliable found (only its own Medium article on Snowflake surfaced).
- Rocket Internet's legacy in North America was not established; see section 5. A 2020 delisting was not confirmed by any result.
- Entrepreneur First fund size and portfolio value ($200M raise, $17B portfolio value) appear only in one aggregator ([f4.fund](https://f4.fund/firms/entrepreneur-first)); not verified.
- Antler's total and US portfolio counts are inconsistent across third-party trackers (766, 991, 1,800+); no official US figure found.

## 2. How many studios exist globally and in North America, and how has the number grown?

### Takeaway
Most 2024–2025 sources put the global count at roughly 950–1,100 studios, with 2024 reported as the first year of net decline (about 154 closures). There is no reliable, consistent North America count: directory figures range from 24 to 117 for Canada, and one directory lists 1,314 for the US alone, which exceeds other sources' global totals.

### Cited Findings
- A University of East Anglia study built a global database of 1,006 studios and estimated growth of about 18% a year. — [UEA news](https://www.uea.ac.uk/about/news/article/the-business-of-building-businesses)
- Over 1,100 active studios worldwide; about 154 studios closed in 2024, described as the first time the total declined (secondary commentary). — [0100 Conferences newsletter](https://0100conferences.substack.com/p/rethinking-venture-building-de-risking)
- A 2024 study analysed 3,452 deals, 1,107 venture studios and 861 outreach responses (this is the Big Venture Studio Research-style dataset; publisher not confirmed in the extract). — [LP Blueprint newsletter](https://metz.substack.com/p/venture-studio-closures-are-risingwhat)
- Enhance's database lists over 950 studios worldwide. — [Enhance](https://enhance.online/ecosystem-building)
- StudioStack's commercial database cites over 500 active studios globally. — [StudioStack](https://studiostack.gumroad.com/l/startup-studio-database)
- The number of global venture studios increased 625% over seven years (as reported by BetaKit). — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/inside-the-rise-of-canadian-venture-studios/)
- Canada: Réseau Capital identified 24 studios and notes the number varies by data source; the SuperScout directory lists 117. — [Réseau Capital](https://reseaucapital.com/en/canadian-venture-studio-landscape/); contradicted by [SuperScout Canada](https://superscout.co/country/canada)
- US: the SuperScout directory lists 1,314 venture studios "on record", a figure larger than other sources' worldwide totals, so its definition is much looser. — [SuperScout United States](https://superscout.co/country/united-states)
- Widely repeated GSSN 2022 figures: 84% of studio startups raise a seed round; 72% reach Series A versus 42% of traditional ventures; 25 months from founding to Series A versus 56 months. — [Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/alternative-investments/how-venture-studios-build-startups); [GoingVC](https://www.goingvc.com/post/the-rise-of-venture-studios)
- The "53% IRR for studio startups vs 21.3% for traditional startups" figure is attributed to GSSN by some sites and to High Alpha by others. — [Forum VC](https://forumvc.com/studio); [GoingVC](https://www.goingvc.com/post/the-rise-of-venture-studios)
- 2026 commentary: studios are described as "no longer an experiment" and entering a refinement phase; generalist studios are expected to struggle. — [startupstudios.com, 2026 outlook](https://startupstudios.com/post/whats-next-for-startup-studios-five-things-to-expect-in-2026)

### Inferences
- A defensible statement for a report: "roughly 1,000 studios worldwide in 2024–2025, up several-fold since the late 2010s, with the first net contraction in 2024".
- The GSSN performance figures (84% / 72% / 53% IRR) are from 2020–2022 samples of mostly successful studios and are repeated by parties with a commercial interest; they should be presented as industry claims, not benchmarks.
- North America's share cannot be stated from the sources found; any percentage in secondary articles should be treated as unverified.

### Gaps
- GSSN's own reports, the Venture Studio Index, Big Venture Studio Research 2024/2025 PDFs, PitchBook and Morrow data could not be opened (network restriction), so their primary numbers (TVPI, IRR, regional split) are not confirmed here.
- No source gave a US or North America share of global studios from a single consistent database.
- No 2026 count of studios was found; a paid "Venture Studio Trends Report 2026" exists but could not be verified ([Payhip listing](https://payhip.com/b/0zwgq)).

## 3. Which studios are vertical-specialised versus generalist, and which are corporate- or university-backed?

### Takeaway
The best-capitalised studios are increasingly vertical: biotech (Flagship), healthcare (Redesign), B2B SaaS (High Alpha, PSL), AI (AI Fund, Betaworks), fintech/climate (Diagram), cybersecurity (Team8). Generalists (Atomic, Expa, Idealab, Wilbur Labs) remain but 2026 commentary expects them to be squeezed. Corporate involvement mostly comes as LP capital or partner-studio models.

### Cited Findings

| Category | Studios (from sources above) |
|---|---|
| Biotech / life sciences | Flagship Pioneering |
| Healthcare | Redesign Health; Team8 digital-health fund |
| B2B SaaS | High Alpha, Pioneer Square Labs, Hexa |
| AI | AI Fund, Betaworks (AI camps), Antler/EF (AI-heavy cohorts) |
| Fintech / climate | Diagram Ventures |
| Cybersecurity / data | Team8 |
| Consumer brands | Science Inc |
| Generalist | Idealab, Atomic, Expa, Wilbur Labs, Human Ventures |
| Corporate-backed or corporate-partner | AI Fund (corporate LPs), 1848 Ventures (Westfield Insurance), Alloy Partners, Coplex, P&G Ventures, Alphabet's X |

- Atomic is described as consumer/SaaS; High Alpha as narrowly B2B SaaS. — [Elev-X 2026](https://elev-x.com/news-insights/article-best-venture-studios/)
- Diagram focuses exclusively on fintech and climate tech. — [f4.fund](https://f4.fund/firms/diagram-ventures)
- AI Fund builds at the application and software-infrastructure layers of the AI stack and has corporate LPs (AES, HP, Mitsui, Mitsubishi, QBE, TELUS). — [AI Fund](https://aifund.ai/insights/ai-fund-closes-oversubscribed-190-million-fund-ii-to-co-found-ai-startups/)
- Corporate studio examples: 1848 Ventures (Westfield Insurance) is cited for operating with real independence; P&G Ventures, Kamet Ventures and Bosch Business Innovations are cited as internal corporate studios; Alphabet's X is listed as a venture-studio example. — [Highline Beta](https://highlinebeta.com/blog/the-best-corporate-venture-studios-10-examples); [Bundl](https://www.bundl.com/knowledge/articles/8-corporate-venture-studio-examples-you-should-know-about-in-2025)
- Alloy Partners (remote-first, North America): a Stanford GSB case describes leadership that helped launch 34 startups and eight venture studios with corporate, university and government partners. — [Stanford GSB case](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/alloy-partners-corporate-venture-building)
- Team8 (Israel) invests across cybersecurity, data/AI, fintech and digital health, with corporate backers such as Walmart and Microsoft in its 2018 raise. — [TechCrunch, 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/23/investors-like-walmart-and-microsoft-back-team8s-cybersecurity-venture-studio-with-85-million)
- Family offices are reported to be using venture studios to build companies. — [Crain Currency](https://www.craincurrency.com/family-office-management/family-offices-quietly-using-venture-studios-build-companies)
- Life-science studios are claimed to outperform traditional VC (trade commentary). — [BioBuzz](https://biobuzz.io/?p=66467)
- A 2026 outlook expects generalist studios to struggle to compete. — [startupstudios.com](https://startupstudios.com/post/whats-next-for-startup-studios-five-things-to-expect-in-2026)

### Inferences
- Vertical focus correlates with the ability to raise large dedicated funds: the three largest capital pools found (Flagship, Team8, Atomic) include two deep verticals.
- Corporate money enters mostly as LP commitments (AI Fund, Team8) or through partner-studio operators (Alloy, Coplex, Highline Beta) rather than wholly-owned corporate studios.

### Gaps
- University-backed studios in North America: no specific, sourced examples were found in this session.
- No data on the share of studios by vertical in North America (the Enhance and startupstudios.com breakdowns could not be opened).
- BCG X, Mach49, FedTech and any Mayo Clinic studio were searched but returned nothing citable.

## 4. Which foreign-headquartered studios launch products into the US market, and how do they organise it?

### Takeaway
The clearest documented cases are Hexa (France), Team8 (Israel), Entrepreneur First and Antler (UK/Singapore hybrids), and the Ukrainian co-founding companies Genesis and SKELAR. The common pattern is a US legal entity and US go-to-market with product and engineering kept at home; Hexa, EF and Antler added a physical San Francisco base in 2024–2026.

### Cited Findings

**Hexa / eFounders (Paris, Brussels)**

### Inferences
- Three organising patterns appear: (a) relocate the founders (EF, Hexa House, Antler residencies); (b) keep R&D at home with US-facing sales and exits to US acquirers (Team8 — all three large exits went to US-headquartered buyers); (c) product-led consumer apps built by a remote home-country team and sold to US users through app stores and paid acquisition (Genesis, SKELAR).

- 2024–2026 shows a clear move by European builders to put physical capacity in San Francisco, driven by AI.

## 5. What notable successes came from studios?

### Takeaway
The largest verified studio-origin outcomes are Snowflake and Pure Storage (Sutter Hill origination), Moderna (Flagship), Hims & Hers (Atomic), Dollar Shave Club (Science), Giphy/Bitly/Dots (Betaworks), Front/Aircall/Spendesk (eFounders/Hexa), Team8's cybersecurity exits, and Rocket Internet's European IPOs. Several attributions in the brief could not be confirmed, and two look incorrect.

### Inferences
- The two largest outcomes (Snowflake, Moderna) came from firms that do not call themselves studios; "origination" by a fund with deep pockets and a partner acting as founding CEO has produced bigger results than classic studios.
- Studio successes cluster where the studio had a strong thesis and domain edge (enterprise infrastructure, mRNA biology, cybersecurity, B2B SaaS), which fits the vertical-specialisation trend in section 3.
