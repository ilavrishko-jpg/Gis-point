# Business models, legal/financial structures and economics of startup studios (North America focus, as of 2026)

> **Method note for the report writer (read first).** Direct page fetches were blocked in this research session (hosts such as focusedchaos.co, hexa.com, highlinebeta.com, fenwick.com, metz.substack.com did not resolve from the sandbox). Every finding below is therefore taken from **search-engine result excerpts/summaries of the linked pages**, not from a full read of the page. Numbers are reproduced exactly as surfaced, but context (sample, definitions, date) could often not be checked against the original. Where a figure is secondary (a blog quoting GSSN, Vault, etc.) this is flagged. ## 1. What are the main studio business-model types and how does each generate revenue and returns?

### Takeaway
There is no standard model: practitioners (Vault Fund) describe studio structures as "fragmented", and the practical split is by *who pays for the building work* — a fund's management fee, a holdco balance sheet, a dual holdco+fund, service revenue, or a corporate sponsor. In all of them, returns come overwhelmingly from equity exits of spinouts; fees/service revenue only cover (part of) operating cost.

### Cited Findings
- **Fund-financed studio (GP/LP).** Studio operations are "financed primarily by management fees from the fund, which in venture capital are typically around 2%"; the model is described by Equidam as still unproven — [Equidam, "The challenge for venture studios"](https://www.equidam.com/the-challenge-for-venture-studios)
- **Why plain 2/20 does not fit.** Highline Beta (Toronto studio operator): "most VCs operate on ~2% management fees and performance carry. That doesn't cut it for studios" — [Highline Beta, "How Do Venture Studios Make Money?"](https://www.highlinebeta.com/blog/launching-a-venture-studio-the-right-way-our-comprehensive-checklist)
- **Holdco / evergreen studio.** "Under the holdco model, holding companies do not have a management fee. Instead, the studio builds companies from the cash on its balance sheet." Holdco structures offer more transparency and pari passu economics for investors but "intimidate traditional LPs, making capital scaling difficult" (Sarah Anderson, Vault Fund) — [Highline Beta podcast with Sarah Anderson, Vault Fund](https://www.highlinebeta.com/podcast/the-future-of-venture-studios-liquidity-structure-scaling-with-sarah-anderson-founding-partner-at-vault-fund)
- **Dual entity (holdco/opco + affiliated fund).** The studio holding entity finances its own operations and also acts as GP of an affiliated fund; each venture's equity is then usually owned by *both* the studio holding entity and the affiliated fund. Hexa (ex-eFounders) calls the hybrid "a great alternative as it solves the limits of management fees" — [Hexa, "Launching a Startup Studio: How to Finance it?"](https://www.hexa.com/blog/news/launching-a-startup-studio-how-to-finance-it)
- **Fund-above-studio variant.** Vault's founder describes a split between an operating studio and a fund above it, with the fund sometimes being the only capital into the studio — [Fast Frontiers podcast S4 Ep2, Sarah Anderson](https://fastfrontiers.refinery.com/public/63/Fast-Frontiers-bafe696f/00912146)
- **Three-entity "cost-billing" model (academic legal description).** A 2025 Case Western Reserve Law Review article (Vol. 76, Issue 2) describes studios that set up a separate investment fund which takes **preferred** shares in spinouts while the studio itself holds **common** shares like a founder; and a three-entity model where the studio bills startups for its costs while founders and the fund hold stock separately — [Case Western Reserve Law Review Vol. 76](https://scholarlycommons.law.case.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5135&context=caselrev)
- **Five-entity stack.** One practitioner Q&A describes a typical studio stack as: management company, GP, LP (fund), the studio/operations entity, and individual portfolio companies — [Decile Group Q&A](https://decilegroup.com/posts/aside-from-management-company-general-partner-limited-partner-what-other-legal-entities-need-created-venture-studio-what-incorporation-structure-recommended-those-entities)
- **Captive / corporate structures.** In captive structures the management fee "is often replaced with a cost-based budget" — [Cooley, The Fund Lawyer](https://thefundlawyer.cooley.com/?p=15037)
- **Corporate venture studio.** Highline Beta estimates an internal corporate studio starts at **$2–5 million before any venture investment** (vendor estimate; Highline sells this service) — [Highline Beta, corporate venture studios](https://www.highlinebeta.com/venture-studios). A Venture Studio Forum governance piece puts corporate-studio ventures at **$500,000–$1 million each**, depending on pace and team size — [Venture Studio Forum, governance framework for corporate studios](https://newsletter.venturestudioforum.org/p/the-governance-framework-for-corporate)
- **Corporate venture building outcomes (McKinsey, self-reported surveys).** 2024 (5th annual survey, 1,176 executives): ~half of CEOs rank new-venture building a top-3 priority; ventures built in the past five years generated **16% of enterprise value and 13% of revenue**; McKinsey suggests allocating **≥20% of growth capital** to venture building; experienced builders report **2x the success rate** of novices — [McKinsey, "How CEOs are turning corporate venture building into outsize growth"](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-digital/our-insights/how-ceos-are-turning-corporate-venture-building-into-outsize-growth). 2025 (6th survey, n=424): larger share of ventures crossing revenue thresholds; weighted-average investment to break-even decreased vs 2024 — [McKinsey, "The way to win in corporate venturing: Serial building and AI"](https://mckinsey.com/capabilities/business-building/our-insights/the-way-to-win-in-corporate-venturing-serial-building-and-ai). Leap practice sample of 35 corporate scale-ups: playbook raised scale-up success rate **to 60% from 22%** (practice sample, not a survey) — [McKinsey, "The big boost"](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/the-big-boost-how-incumbents-successfully-scale-their-new-businesses)
- **Accelerator-hybrid / "day-zero investor" (Antler).** No fee to join the US residency; Antler takes equity only if it invests after an investment-committee pitch. July 2023 US terms: **$250K at $2.75M post-money = 9.09%**, plus $2,500 relocation grant, 8-week residency (2025 cohorts ~6 weeks; Fall 2025 ran Sept 30–Nov 7, 2025). Separate "Disrupt deal": $250,000 for 10% — [Antler, revamped US terms](https://www.antler.co/blog/antler-reveals-revamped-us-investment-terms); [Antler Disrupt deal](https://antler.co/blog/the-antler-disrupt-deal); [Built In coverage](https://builtin.com/articles/antler-new-investment-terms). Secondary guides say newer Antler material cites $500K–$1M initial commitments and no fixed % (unverified) — [elev-x review](https://elev-x.com/news-insights/article-antler-accelerator-review/)
- **Terminology.** Vault Fund (an LP/fund-of-studios) switched from "venture studio" to **"company creation fund"** because the older term was ambiguous; it defines a "company creator" as an entity that serves as founder or co-founder across its portfolio — [Vault Fund, Updated Terminology](https://vaultfund.com/updated-terminology-company-creation-funds/)
- **Market size/concentration (Big Startup Studios Research 2023, Max Pog; secondary summaries).** 877 studios in 2023 (doubled since 2018); studios have raised **$21B**, with the top 5 studios receiving half and the top 20 receiving 80%; 67% of money went to single-focus studios though they are 36% of studios — [inniches, Big Startup Studios Research 2023](https://inniches.com/startup-studios-research); 2024 edition: [inniches, Big Venture Studio Research 2024](https://inniches.com/big-venture-studio-research) (described elsewhere as covering 3,452 deals, 1,107 studios, 861 outreach responses — [Nicolas Dolenc Substack](https://nicolasdolenc.substack.com/p/just-thoughts-37-venture-studio-research)). Wikipedia cites a 2026 study counting 1,006 studios, ~18% annual growth — [Wikipedia, Startup studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_studio)
- **Studio funds as a share of new VC funds (emerging-manager data, Founder Institute/VC Lab).** In 2024, studio funds were 10.3% of all VC funds launched in that dataset (nearly 2x accelerator funds); average *minimum* target fund size $5.8M (vs $7.1M accelerator funds); average early soft commitments $2.8M in first 6–12 months — [GoVCLab, studio vs accelerator funds (Mar 2025)](https://govclab.com/2025/03/17/venture-studio-versus-accelerator-funds/); [GoVCLab, Aug 2025](https://govclab.com/2025/08/25/the-surge-of-venture-studio-funds-in-emerging-vc/). Note: this is a sample of VC Lab emerging managers, not the whole market.

### Inferences
- A workable taxonomy for the report (synthesised from the above, not from a single source): (a) fund-financed builder studio (fees + carry + GP commit; equity via fund); (b) holdco/evergreen (investors buy studio shares; no fee; returns via dividends/buybacks/holdco exit); (c) dual holdco + sidecar/affiliated fund (holdco gets founder-like common, fund buys preferred); (d) agency / sweat-equity studio (service revenue + small equity); (e) corporate studio / studio-as-a-service (corporate pays build budget of ~$0.5–1M per venture or $2–5M/yr programme; operator may get fees + equity); (f) accelerator-hybrid (Antler: standardised ~9–10% for $250K, no building team).
- Revenue vs returns: only (d) and (e) have meaningful current revenue; (a)–(c) are essentially long-duration equity bets subsidised by fees or balance sheet, which is why capital concentration is extreme (top 20 studios = 80% of money raised).

### Gaps
- No source retrieved that quantifies the *share* of studios using each structure (GSSN/Morrow/Vault surveys likely contain it; originals not accessible). No Morrow report was found at all.
- Agency/"sweat equity" and studio-as-a-service economics (day rates, cash/equity mix) — no reliable sourced numbers found. Mach49, Rule 1 Ventures, Enhance, FutureSight, PSL and Atomic operator essays could not be retrieved.
- Bain, HBR and Kauffman Fellows material on studios not retrieved.

## 2. How much equity do studios take at formation, how does it dilute, what do recruited CEOs get, and how do investors view studio cap tables?

### Takeaway
Reported studio stakes at formation span ~15% to 80%, with the frequently quoted GSSN survey average at 34%; the direction of travel is downward — Vault Fund says the best studios are settling near ~25% (vs 80–90% in the model's early days) and its 2023 data paper reports average targets of 21–43%. Downstream VCs treat large, passive studio stakes as a cap-table red flag; rule-of-thumb expectation is that founders/management hold a majority through Series A.

### Cited Findings
- **GSSN survey average: 34% studio stake at founding** (cited by GeekWire, 2021). Pioneer Square Labs (Seattle) took ~40%+ when it started and has since reduced its initial take — [GeekWire, PSL spun out 25 ideas in 5 years (2021)](https://www.geekwire.com/2021/seattle-startup-studio-pioneer-square-labs-spun-25-ideas-5-years-way/)
- **Vault Fund (2025 podcast):** best studios now aim for **~25%** ownership, down from **80–90%** stakes common when Vault began investing; high ownership is associated with weaker founders and harder downstream fundraising — [Highline Beta podcast, Sarah Anderson](https://www.highlinebeta.com/podcast/the-future-of-venture-studios-liquidity-structure-scaling-with-sarah-anderson-founding-partner-at-vault-fund)
- **Vault Fund 2023 Company Creator Insights:** studios' *target* ownership averages **between 21% and 43%**, "largely dependent on structure and funding"; Vault sees no general correlation between structure and performance but possibly between size and performance — [Vault Fund, 2023 Company Creator Insights](https://vaultfund.com/2023-company-creator-insights-data-paper/). On a podcast the founder gave a wider **30–60% at formation** range — [Fast Frontiers podcast](https://fastfrontiers.refinery.com/public/63/Fast-Frontiers-bafe696f/00912146)
- **"Thirds" benchmark:** optimal split ~**30% each for studio, founders, outside investors** (Sarah Anderson interview) — [Ignite LP interview](https://teamignite.vc/blog/ignite-lp-the-secrets-behind-high)
- **Named-studio stakes (secondary blogs, not confirmed by the studios):** Atomic ~40–60% (or 40–50%) at incorporation; High Alpha ~20–30% plus follow-on reserve — [TFSF Ventures, equity math](https://www.tfsfventures.com/blog/the-equity-math-behind-venture-studios-what-founders-actually-give-up); hands-on studios "commonly 25–50%" — [Alloy Partners directory (2026)](https://www.alloypartners.com/articles/venture-building-companies)
- **Ranges quoted in guides:** 15–80% — [OpenVC](https://openvc.app/blog/startup-studios); 30–50% of initial equity typical — [GoingVC](https://www.goingvc.com/post/the-rise-of-venture-studios). An unattributed data point: median 17%, IQR 12–23% — [Codiste](https://www.codiste.com/common-mistakes-venture-studios-make) (source of data unclear; treat with caution).
- **Dilution modelling.** Ben Yoskovitz (Highline Beta) model: studio starts at **20%** (company valued ~$5M at that point), diluted to **~15% after seed rounds**, assuming no follow-on — [Focused Chaos, "Understanding Venture Studio Math"](https://www.focusedchaos.co/p/venture-studio-math). Worked example: founders starting at 75% vs 60% (i.e., studio taking 25% vs 40%) and selling 20% per round end at 60%/48% post-seed and 48%/38% post-Series A — [Alder VC blog](https://aldervc.pages.dev/blog/venture-studio-equity/)
- **Non-studio baseline (Carta).** Founding teams hold ~**56% after seed and ~36% after Series A**; median dilution ~19% at seed and 17.9% at Series A (early 2025; Series A was 20.9% a year earlier) — [Carta Founder Ownership Report 2025](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership/); only ~1 in 5 founding teams hold 50% after Series A — [Carta](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-founders-rarely-majority-after-series-a/)
- **Investor view.** "VCs want to see the founding team own the majority of the equity into and past their Series A"; studio-incubated startups with large studio stakes are often turned away — [Founders in the Cloud, "Protect Ya Cap Table"](https://foundersinthecloud.beehiiv.com/p/protect-ya-cap-table). Heuristic in a founder-oriented piece: studio >20% at idea stage → likely uninvestable (opinion) — same source cluster; "5%+ held by people no longer involved" is a major red flag — [Persuade and Raise](https://persuade-and-raise.beehiiv.com/p/red-flags-on-cap-tables); Fenwick defines dead equity as large stakes held by inactive/departed holders — [Fenwick, "What is dead equity"](https://whatstrending.fenwick.com/post/what-is-dead-equity)
- **Follow-on makes it worse.** The downstream cap-table concern "will be exacerbated if the venture studio does follow on and retains a big equity stake" — [Focused Chaos](https://www.focusedchaos.co/p/venture-studio-math)
- **Fenwick/Gitwit model:** Fenwick's work with Gitwit (Tulsa studio) is described as designed to avoid the "dead capital" problem that deters Series A investors — [Fenwick, Byron Dailey and Gitwit](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/byron-dailey-and-gitwit-discuss-their-development-of-a-new-venture-studio-model)
- **Academic warning:** founders "must be wary of losing control of their new venture" in exchange for studio resources — [Case Western Reserve Law Review Vol. 76 (2025)](https://scholarlycommons.law.case.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5135&context=caselrev)

### Inferences
- Combining Carta's baseline (founders 36% post-A) with a 25–34% formation stake for the studio and ~20% dilution per round: a studio at 30% falls to ~24% post-seed and ~19% post-A; the recruited founding team at ~55–60% (after a ~10–15% pool) falls to ~35–38% post-A — at or below the "founder ownership under 40% by Series A is risky" heuristic quoted in cap-table guides ([Qubit Capital](https://qubit.capital/?p=65231) — attribution to this page is probable but not verified). This arithmetic explains the market convergence toward ~20–30% studio stakes for US venture-track spinouts.
- The 30–80% "founder-studio" vs 15–35% "co-founding studio" ranges in the brief are consistent with the sources (Atomic-type 40–60%; High Alpha-type 20–30%; Vault "best practice" ~25%), but the high end (>50%) is now dated practice for studios that expect US Series A investors.
- The widely circulated "60% of VCs reject studio startups" figure could not be traced to a study; do not use.

### Gaps
- No reliable data on what recruited CEOs/co-founders receive (typical % grants, salary from day one). Only an unquantified note that PSL founders are paid from day one. Operator disclosures (Atomic, High Alpha, PSL, Hexa) were not retrievable.
- No Carta/PitchBook cut of *studio-originated* cap tables at seed/Series A was found; it may not exist publicly.
- Researcher's unverified background recollection (NOT sourced here; verify before use): Hexa/eFounders historically started around 30% studio stake, diluting to roughly 15–20% by Series A; Atomic has raised funds in the $260–320M range.

## 3. How are studios financed; what are typical fund sizes, operating budgets and cost per company launched?

### Takeaway
Median studio operating budget reported by GSSN (2022) is about $1.36M/year (mean $2.49M); cost to get one company to external-investment readiness is repeatedly quoted at roughly $200K–$1M, with GSSN's average starting capital of $476K per company as the most-cited single figure. Funding ops purely from a 2% fee requires a ≥$50M fund, which is why small studios use holdco, dual structures, elevated fees/cost budgets or services revenue.

### Cited Findings
- **Operating budget (GSSN Data Report 2022, via secondary blog):** median annual studio budget **$1.36M**, average **$2.49M** — [Kevin Monserrat blog](https://www.kevinmonserrat.com/a-bigger-fund-wont-fix-your-venture-studio-more-companies-per-dollar-will/)
- **Fee arithmetic:** covering ~$1M/yr operating cost from a 2% fee requires a fund of **at least $50M** — same source — [Kevin Monserrat blog](https://www.kevinmonserrat.com/a-bigger-fund-wont-fix-your-venture-studio-more-companies-per-dollar-will/)
- **Starting capital per company (GSSN, secondary):** 79% of studios provide starting capital, averaging **$476K per company**; the same piece notes the survey drew on ~40 studios with a survivorship-bias concern — [exventure, "Is a Venture Studio Worth It?"](https://exventure.co/news/is-a-venture-studio-worth-it-for-founders)
- **Per-venture budget to investment readiness: $200K–$1M** — [Hemrock (Foresight), "Venture Studios: How the Model and the Economics Work"](https://www.hemrock.com/docs/venture-studios)
- **Venture Studio Forum illustrative model (hypothetical 10-company studio):** ~$270K cost per company; allocation per company of $74K overhead, $296K builds, $30K common-stock purchase plus $500K preferred investment — [Venture Studio Forum, "The Cost of Company Creation"](https://newsletter.venturestudioforum.org/p/the-cost-of-company-creation). Venture Studio Index cases: **$600K per company** with 70% of capital going to direct building in one case; **$900K** in another — [Venture Studio Forum, "The Venture Studio Index"](https://newsletter.venturestudioforum.org/p/the-venture-studio-index)
- **Single-studio math example:** ~$500K/yr operating cost, 6–8 companies from $6M over four years — [Builders Studio (Medium)](https://medium.com/builders-studio/the-math-behind-our-startup-studio-cfa4f4b857ff)
- **Pitch-deck claim citing Vault pipeline data:** total cost per spinout = **$817K** (a fundraiser's claim, not a Vault publication) — [InsightStudios pitch deck](https://s3.amazonaws.com/insightstudios/InsightStudios+Pitch+Deck.pdf)
- **Capital to start a studio:** "$2M to $10M" per various experts — [Hemrock](https://www.hemrock.com/docs/venture-studios); "at least $1–2M or, better, $5–10M+" — [Flagship Founders LinkedIn post on Big Startup Studios Research 2023](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/flagshipfounders_big-startup-studios-research-2023-activity-7130835456170057728-MAPl?trk=public_profile_like_view)
- **Studio fund sizes.** Emerging-manager studio funds target leaner sizes (~$6.4M vs $10.6M for traditional funds in the VC Lab sample; average minimum $5.8M) — [fi.co / VC Lab](https://fi.co/insight/the-rise-of-venture-studio-funds-in-emerging-vc); [GoVCLab](https://govclab.com/2025/03/17/venture-studio-versus-accelerator-funds/). Established US studios are far larger: High Alpha has raised four funds and >$300M AUM — [Alloy Partners directory](https://www.alloypartners.com/articles/venture-building-companies) (secondary); PSL's studio entity had raised $27.5M as of 2021 — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2021/seattle-startup-studio-pioneer-square-labs-spun-25-ideas-5-years-way/)
- **Standard fund terms as baseline:** 2–2.5% management fee, 20% carry — [Sydecar, industry standard terms](https://sydecar.io/blog/industry-standard-terms)
- **Investor-level economics in holdco/studio entity:** "ownership split is often 25% to 50% for investors with the balance going to the general partners and staff of the studio" — [Redesigning Entrepreneurship: A Guide to Investing in Startup Studios (2023 PDF)](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/62ebbe72de40e55dfc232dd8/64c2233eae8773a0068b0693_July%2021%20Redesigning%20Entrepreneurship_compressed.pdf)
- **Management company vs GP separation:** fee = compensation for running the firm (management company, employs staff); carry = allocation of profit (GP entity); mixing them complicates carry splits — [Allocations, GP entity vs management company](https://www.allocations.com/insights/gp-entity-vs-management-company)
- **Fee-offset trap:** if the studio/GP earns service or board fees from portfolio companies, a poorly drafted offset clause automatically reduces management fees; DLA Piper partner Yoni Tuchman recommends explicit carve-outs for arm's-length services — [VC10X podcast with Yoni Tuchman, DLA Piper](https://vc10x.beehiiv.com/p/the-legal-landmines-hiding-in-your-fund-docs-yoni-tuchman-partner-dla-piper)

### Inferences
- "Cost per company launched" is definitional: (annual opex ÷ companies launched) plus direct pre-seed cheque. With GSSN's median $1.36M budget and 2–4 launches/yr, loaded cost is ~$350–700K per spinout before the pre-seed cheque — consistent with the $600–900K (Venture Studio Index) and $817K figures, and with the brief's "$250K–$1M+" range.
- Sub-$50M studios cannot run a full building team on 2% fees; they need a holdco raise, elevated/cost-budget fees, GP-funded opex, or service revenue. This is the structural reason dual-entity structures dominate among newer US studios (inference from fee arithmetic + Hexa/Vault commentary).

### Gaps
- No verified figures for elevated studio-fund fees (e.g., 2.5–3%+ or "budget-based" fees) or for carry splits between studio opco and fund GP; a targeted search returned nothing specific.
- Fund sizes for Atomic and other large US studios not retrieved from a citable source.
- GSSN original report not accessible; all GSSN budget numbers are second-hand.

## 4. What performance data exists and how reliable is it?

### Takeaway
The headline statistics (53% vs 21.3% IRR; 84% reach seed; 72% seed→Series A vs 42%; 25.2 vs 56 months to Series A) all trace to GSSN's self-reported survey work (2020 "Disrupting the Venture Landscape" / 2022 data report), with a sample reportedly of ~40 studios / 200–258 startups and acknowledged survivorship bias. They are endlessly recycled by studio marketing; independent, audited benchmarks (PitchBook, Cambridge Associates-style) do not appear to exist publicly.

### Cited Findings
- **IRR claim:** studio startups average **53% IRR vs 21.3%** for traditional startups; secondary sources attribute it to the GSSN 2022 Startup Studio Data Report "as compiled by Alloy Partners", others to GSSN's 2020 white paper; one attributes the 21% to a different source — [Bundl](https://www.bundl.com/articles/why-venture-studio-startups-have-higher-long-term-success-rates); [inniches Big Startup Studios Research 2023](https://inniches.com/startup-studios-research)
- **Graduation/time claims (GSSN):** **84%** of studio startups raise seed; **72%** of those reach Series A (vs **42%** traditional); ~**60%** inception→Series A; **25.2 months** zero→Series A vs **56 months** — [valu.vc, "The Venture Studio Model: History, Economics and the Winners"](https://valu.vc/venture-studio-model-history/); [Mandalore Partners (2025)](https://www.mandalorepartners.com/en/research/why-venture-studios-are-attracting-more-investors-in-2025) (these figures were surfaced across a cluster of secondary pages incl. valu.vc, Mandalore, exventure, thestartupvc; exact page-to-figure mapping not verified; attribution wavers between GSSN 2020 and GSSN 2022)
- **Sample/reliability:** described variously as a GSSN survey of **258 studio-created startups** or "200+ companies"; an audit cited in one piece says it drew on **~40 studios** and documents a survivorship-bias concern; top studios drive most outperformance — [exventure](https://exventure.co/news/is-a-venture-studio-worth-it-for-founders); [valu.vc](https://valu.vc/venture-studio-vs-venture-capital/)
- **Big Startup Studios Research 2023 (Max Pog):** studio startups reach seed ~2x faster (**1.49 vs 3.03 years**) and exit **33% faster** than conventional startups; repeats the 84% and 53%/21.3% figures — [inniches](https://inniches.com/startup-studios-research). Other secondary figures: time to seed 10.6–11 months ("2.27x faster") — [OpenVC](https://openvc.app/blog/startup-studios) (could not confirm these originate in Pog's study)
- **Vault Fund data:** LinkedIn summary of its 2023 report cites **33% net IRR** as the top-quartile traditional-venture comparator; a secondary newsletter attributes to Vault **average net IRRs of 60%** for company creators vs 33% — [Sarah Anderson LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sarah-adams-anderson-b6231916_2023-company-creator-insights-vault-fund-activity-7130988208040906753-oq8a); [Venture Studio Forum, "The Fatal Flaws in the Venture Studio Model"](https://newsletter.venturestudioforum.org/p/the-fatal-flaws-in-the-venture-studio) (60% not confirmed in Vault's own text; Vault is itself a studio-focused LP with selected sample)
- **Sceptical views:** "not much credible information out there on venture studio funds" — [Decile Group](https://decilegroup.com/posts/are-there-any-recent-studies-done-around-venture-studio-funds); studio-favourable numbers "come largely from studio-affiliated research and industry associations with an obvious interest in the answer" — [exventure](https://exventure.co/news/is-a-venture-studio-worth-it-for-founders); peer-reviewed critique: "Venture studios beyond the hype: Key challenges and a way forward" (Business Horizons, 2025) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0007681325001417); unsourced claim that "90% of venture studios fail to return meaningful capital" (advocacy-style article; weigh accordingly) — [M Accelerator](https://maccelerator.la/en/?p=42703)
- **Closures:** a 2025–2026 LP-oriented newsletter reports studio closures rising — [LP Blueprint (Metz), "Venture studio closures are rising"](https://metz.substack.com/p/venture-studio-closures-are-risingwhat) (content not retrievable; title only)

### Inferences
- The 53% IRR figure is a *company-level* (per-startup, gross) metric from a small voluntary survey of network members in 2010s vintage years; it is not a net-to-LP fund IRR and should never be presented as such. ## 5. What legal structures are standard for US-market spinouts and what terms do studios use?

### Takeaway
Spinouts follow ordinary US venture practice — a Delaware C-corp per NewCo with founder common stock on 4-year/1-year-cliff vesting and full IP assignment — with studio-specific overlays: the studio (opco/holdco) holds founder-like common stock, an affiliated fund buys preferred, and a services agreement governs studio work billed or contributed to the NewCo. Studio-specific law-firm guidance is thin in public sources; the best academic treatment found is a 2025 Case Western Reserve Law Review article.

### Cited Findings
- **Common/preferred split between studio and fund:** studios set up a separate investment fund that takes preferred shares while the studio holds common shares "like a founder would"; alternative three-entity model in which the studio bills startups for its costs — [Case Western Reserve Law Review Vol. 76, Issue 2 (2025)](https://scholarlycommons.law.case.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5135&context=caselrev) (also hosted at [Gana law firm](https://www.ganalawfirm.com/files/gana_article_pub_edit.pdf))
- **Counsel conflicts:** outside counsel may need to disclose conflicts to both the studio and the new venture (joint representation issue) — same article — [Case Western Reserve Law Review Vol. 76](https://scholarlycommons.law.case.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5135&context=caselrev)
- **Illustrative capitalisation at formation:** $30K common-stock purchase plus $500K preferred investment per company in the Venture Studio Forum model — [Venture Studio Forum, "The Cost of Company Creation"](https://newsletter.venturestudioforum.org/p/the-cost-of-company-creation)
- **Delaware C-corp default** for venture-backed companies (dual-class common/preferred capability, Court of Chancery case law); founder vesting standard 4 years with 1-year cliff; IP must be assigned to the company by every founder, with CIIAAs for employees and IP-assignment clauses for contractors; timely 83(b) elections — [Morgan Lewis, "Launching the high-growth startup" (June 2026)](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/06/launching-the-high-growth-startup-key-legal-and-financing-considerations); [Promise Legal startup guide](https://blog.promise.legal/startup-central/startup-legal-guide-entity-formation-equity-fundraising-contracts-ip-for-early-stage-founders/)
- **Studio governance terms:** typical studio board seat and veto rights over major decisions (vendor blog, not legal authority) — [Padiso, "Co-Founder Agreements When a Studio Holds Equity"](https://www.padiso.co/blog/co-founder-agreements-venture-studio-model/)
- **Pro rata / board mechanics** follow NVCA model documents — [NVCA Model Term Sheet](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/NVCA-Model-Term-Sheet-1.doc)
- **Shared-services agreements:** documented precedent in public filings — cost-sharing for IT, admin and staff under a shared services agreement — [Field Trip Health Form 40-F exhibit (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1865482/000110465922075940/ftrp-20220629xex99d1.htm)
- **Dual-representation / anti-dead-capital design:** Fenwick's Gitwit studio model is positioned as avoiding the "dead capital" problem for Series A investors — [Fenwick, Byron Dailey and Gitwit](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/byron-dailey-and-gitwit-talk-about-their-venture-studios-collaboration)
- **Due-diligence checklist for founders joining studios** — [StartupStudios.com, "Measure Twice, Sign Once"](https://www.startupstudios.com/post/measure-twice-sign-once-a-founders-due-diligence-guide-for-startup-studios); law-firm overview — [Signature Block, "Venture studios & incubations"](https://signatureblock.co/articles/venture-studios) (contents not retrieved)

### Inferences
- A "standard" US studio spinout package, assembled from the above (synthesis, not a single cited template): (1) Delaware C-corp NewCo; (2) studio entity buys founder common at par (cheap stock, e.g., ~$30K), sometimes with studio-level vesting or milestone/repurchase mechanics to answer the dead-equity objection; (3) recruited CEO/CTO get restricted common on 4-yr/1-yr-cliff vesting with 83(b); (4) IP developed by studio staff assigned to NewCo at formation (assignment agreement); (5) studio fund (or holdco) invests pre-seed via SAFE/preferred ($250K–$1M) with pro rata rights and a board seat; (6) shared-services/master services agreement for studio staff time, at cost or contributed in exchange for equity, with a sunset; (7) fund LPA carve-out so services fees don't trigger fee offsets.
- Studio vesting on its own common stock and time-limited services agreements are the main structural answers to the "dead equity" critique — supported directionally by the Fenwick/Gitwit framing, but not confirmed term-by-term in retrieved sources.

### Gaps
- No Cooley, Gunderson or Orrick article specifically on studio spinout structuring was found; Fenwick's two pieces exist but were not retrievable in full. Specific term-sheet clauses (studio anti-dilution, ROFR/co-sale, studio share vesting schedules, services-agreement pricing, QSBS planning for studio common held through LLC vs C-corp) remain unsourced.
- Canadian specifics (CCPC status, SR&ED, Canadian vs Delaware incorporation for Canadian studios) not researched.

## 6. How do LPs and downstream VCs evaluate studios; what are the known conflicts?

### Takeaway
LPs focus on structure-level alignment — which entity owns the cheap founder equity vs the paid preferred, what fees fund, and whether LP look-through ownership is diluted by a holdco layer — while downstream VCs focus on founder ownership and whether the studio's stake is "earning" or dead. The specialist LP (Vault Fund) says there is no standard structure and that fund structures often hide misalignment.

### Cited Findings
- **Fee/asset mismatch:** fund structures "often hide critical misalignments such as using 2% management fees to fund discovery work while only including post-launch equity in the fund" (i.e., LPs pay for ideation, but the cheap founder equity sits in a different entity) — Sarah Anderson, Vault Fund — [Highline Beta podcast](https://www.highlinebeta.com/podcast/the-future-of-venture-studios-liquidity-structure-scaling-with-sarah-anderson-founding-partner-at-vault-fund)
- **Look-through ownership:** in fund structures "the pass-through ownership that LPs can reach is usually not as advantageous"; holdcos give pari passu economics and transparency but scare traditional LPs and are hard to scale; Vault has invested in both fund and holdco models — same source — [Highline Beta podcast](https://www.highlinebeta.com/podcast/the-future-of-venture-studios-liquidity-structure-scaling-with-sarah-anderson-founding-partner-at-vault-fund)
- **No structure–performance correlation** observed by Vault generally; possible size–performance correlation — [Vault Fund 2023 Company Creator Insights](https://vaultfund.com/2023-company-creator-insights-data-paper/)
- **LP unfamiliarity:** LPs unfamiliar with the model may view it as niche; fund economics can conflict with studio ownership — [GoVCLab (Aug 2025)](https://govclab.com/2025/08/25/the-surge-of-venture-studio-funds-in-emerging-vc/)
- **Non-market fees are a hard sell:** raising the management fee above market is difficult because investors resist non-market terms — [Equidam](https://www.equidam.com/the-challenge-for-venture-studios)
- **Service-fee conflicts:** question of whether a studio can provide operational support "without creating conflicts with founders and investors through consulting fees and charge-backs" (from a sponsor press release; framing, not evidence) — [pubt.io release](https://www.pubt.io/view/09ABBB4CC51772191F6CC54D3D4F7C15BBE4ADF4); fee-offset drafting issue — [VC10X / DLA Piper](https://vc10x.beehiiv.com/p/the-legal-landmines-hiding-in-your-fund-docs-yoni-tuchman-partner-dla-piper)
- **Illiquidity/duration critique** and structural-flaw critiques from within the studio community — [Venture Studio Forum, "The Illiquid Index Trap"](https://newsletter.venturestudioforum.org/p/the-illiquid-index-trap); ["The Fatal Flaws in the Venture Studio Model"](https://newsletter.venturestudioforum.org/p/the-fatal-flaws-in-the-venture-studio) (titles surfaced; arguments not retrieved in detail)
- **Who invests in studios** (LP landscape) — [Jake Hurwitz, "Who is investing in studios?"](https://thecofounder.substack.com/p/who-is-investing-in-studios) (content not retrieved); family-office appetite study — [inniches, Family Office × Venture Studio Research 2025](https://inniches.com/family-office-venture-studio-research-2025) (content not retrieved)
- **Downstream VC view** — founder majority through Series A expected; large or passive studio stakes are a red flag; follow-on by the studio aggravates the concern (see Q2 sources) — [Founders in the Cloud](https://foundersinthecloud.beehiiv.com/p/protect-ya-cap-table); [Focused Chaos](https://www.focusedchaos.co/p/venture-studio-math)
- **Studio fiduciary/counsel conflicts** between studio and NewCo — [Case Western Reserve Law Review Vol. 76](https://scholarlycommons.law.case.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5135&context=caselrev)

### Inferences
- The "double fee" problem has two layers: (i) LP pays management fee to the fund *and* the fund's capital pays the studio opco (services/charge-backs) for building; (ii) in fund-of-studios vehicles (e.g., Vault) there is an additional fee layer. Neither was quantified in retrieved sources; the conflict is documented qualitatively only.
- LP diligence checklist implied by sources: where does founder common sit (GP-owned opco vs LP-owned vehicle)? What % of committed capital reaches companies vs opex (Venture Studio Index case: 70% to direct building)? Studio ownership at formation and at Series A; graduation rates verified by third-party rounds rather than internal mark-ups; key-person concentration; follow-on policy; service-fee offsets.
- The 2025–2026 environment (reported rise in studio closures; Vault's "best studios ~25%" observation) suggests the market is consolidating around fewer, larger, lower-ownership studios.

- No sourced quantification of "double fees".
