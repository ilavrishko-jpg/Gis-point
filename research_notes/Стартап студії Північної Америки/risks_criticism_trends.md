# Startup / Venture Studio Model: Criticisms, Failure Modes, Risks and Trends 2024-2026 (North America focus)

Research date: 2026-11-19. Method note for the report writer: page fetching was blocked in this session (techcrunch.com, geekwire.com, metz.substack.com, startupstudios.com did not resolve), so every finding below rests on search-result summaries of the linked pages, not on a full read of the article. Where a search summary did not make clear which of several pages carried a statement, the attribution is marked "attribution approximate". Figures labelled "self-reported" come from studios or studio-industry bodies.

## 1. What are the main criticisms of studios, and who voices them?

### Takeaway
The best-documented criticism is the cap-table one: large studio stakes for little cash are treated by later investors as "dead equity" and depress valuations or kill rounds; the industry itself now concedes this, with leading studios targeting about 25% rather than the historical 80-90%. The second is evidentiary: the headline performance numbers (53% IRR vs 21%, faster Series A) are self-reported by a studio industry body and have no independent replication; the only peer-reviewed study found does not measure IRR.

### Cited Findings
**Cap table / "uninvestable" spinouts**
- Sarah Anderson (Vault Fund, an LP in studios) says the best studios now target ~25% ownership, down from a historical 80-90%, because excessive ownership correlates with weaker founders and harder downstream fundraising (opinion of an investor in studios; podcast) — [Highline Beta podcast](https://highlinebeta.com/podcast/the-future-of-venture-studios-liquidity-structure-scaling-with-sarah-anderson-founding-partner-at-vault-fund)
- Highline Beta's 2025 outlook puts the average studio stake at ~25% and names cap-table structure a key risk (studio's own commentary) — [Highline Beta](https://www.highlinebeta.com/blog/what-should-founders-investors-operators-expect-from-venture-studios-in-2025-and-beyond)
- GSSN (2020 report, as cited by High Alpha) put the average studio stake at 34%, with top stakes near 80% (self-reported, industry body) — [High Alpha](https://highalpha.com/why-venture-studios-are-the-future-of-company-building/)
- A VC-lawyer/investor blog argues studios "routinely" take 40%+ and lists heavy studio/incubator ownership among cap-table red flags that can kill a deal (opinion, 2023 and July 2024) — [Tremendous: The venture studio trap](https://tremendous.blog/2023/07/14/the-venture-studio-trap); [Tremendous: Four cap table red flags](https://tremendous.blog/2024/07/31/four-cap-table-red-flags-that-can-kill-a-deal)
- Quebec's VC association (Réseau Capital) notes studio equity can be perceived as "dead equity" on the cap table, and that some Series A/B investors have become sceptical of heavily studio-owned cap tables (attribution approximate) — [Réseau Capital](https://reseaucapital.com/en/challenges-or-limitations-of-venture-studios/)
- Fenwick (law firm) advises studio companies "don't look weird" to downstream VCs, and describes the "Tulsa Model" (Gitwit) as fixing misaligned cap tables by separating the studio's co-founder role from its investor role — [Fenwick: Don't look weird](https://whatstrending.fenwick.com/post/dont-look-weird); [Fenwick/Gitwit](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/byron-dailey-and-gitwit-discuss-their-development-of-a-new-venture-studio-model)
- Reported equity range across models is 10%-80%; a "60% of VCs reject venture studio startups" claim circulates in a video summary but its source could not be traced — treat as unverified — [Pulse: How much equity](https://www.pulse.bot/entrepreneurship/video/how-much-equity-do-venture-studios-take-6f46acdf-48dc-4dd1-9ab6-e90825c4d428/); [Pulse: Why 60% of VCs reject](https://www.pulse.bot/venture-capital/video/why-60-of-vcs-reject-venture-studio-startups-bb6e62bc-53ae-485f-9526-6cc70568b7e0/)
- Founder sentiment (anecdotal, Reddit r/startups teardowns, 2026): a permanent 60% partner makes a company effectively unfundable at Series A; above 10-15% for early support the cost of capital is seen as too high; "if you don't need external capital, studio equity is usually the most expensive way to pay for product work" — [Discury: equity demands](https://discury.io/digest/teardown-venture-studio-equity-demands-3942); [Discury: service fees and value proposition](https://discury.io/digest/teardown-venture-studio-service-fees-and-value-proposition-d253)
- Practitioner view (Ben Yoskovitz, Focused Chaos): the more equity a studio takes, the more capital and services it must provide; a studio that invests no capital is a major problem — [Focused Chaos: good deal?](https://www.focusedchaos.co/p/good-venture-studio-deal); [Focused Chaos: studio math](https://www.focusedchaos.co/p/venture-studio-math)
- Benchmark for comparison: the median digital startup founding team retains 37.5% after Series A (Carta-based figure cited in a studio-related source; attribution approximate) — [SignatureBlock](https://signatureblock.co/articles/venture-studios)

**Inflated / self-reported statistics, survivorship bias**
- The widely repeated figures — 53% average IRR for studio startups vs ~21% for traditional startups; Series A in ~25 months vs 56 — originate with GSSN (Global Startup Studio Network) reports from 2020/2022 and are cited second-hand by studios (self-reported, industry body) — [High Alpha](https://highalpha.com/why-venture-studios-are-the-future-of-company-building/); [Bundl](https://www.bundl.com/articles/why-venture-studio-startups-have-higher-long-term-success-rates)
- "84% of studio startups raise seed; 72% reach Series A vs 42% of traditional ventures" appears with no methodology (vendor content) — [Bundl](https://www.bundl.com/articles/why-venture-studio-startups-have-higher-long-term-success-rates)
- Commentators warn that these figures come largely from studio sources, that the producing organisations have an incentive, that datasets are small and subject to survivorship bias, and that better follow-on funding may reflect selection (studios choosing larger markets) rather than causation; one also cites a less-quoted claim that ~70% of studios themselves fail (unsourced; attribution approximate) — [GoingVC](https://www.goingvc.com/post/the-rise-of-venture-studios); [Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/alternative-investments/how-venture-studios-build-startups); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/studios/article-383340)
- Only peer-reviewed evidence found: Patel & Chan (2024, journal *Venture Capital* 26(3)), 350 studios in 34 countries founded 1994-2022; studio-level differences explain ~30% of variance in venture outcomes. It does not measure IRR and the authors frame it as a first step, not proof of superior returns — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/veecee/v26y2024i3p283-301.html)
- General VC benchmark bias: voluntary reporting skews datasets upward (estimated 200-500 bps overstatement) and IRR is highly sensitive to early cash flows (attribution approximate) — [VCBeast: benchmark bias](https://vcbeast.com/venture-capital-glossary/benchmark-bias)

**Overhead, blurred category, conflicts**
- Andy Budd (March 2026): many self-described studios are really agencies using fee-paying client work to subsidise internal ventures — [Andy Budd](https://andybudd.com/archives/2026/03/will-ai-super-charge-venture-studios)
- The "studio" label covers at least three economically different businesses (opinion) — [Discury](https://discury.io/digest/teardown-venture-studio-service-fees-and-value-proposition-d253)
- Founders are advised to check the full equity picture including future stakes the studio's affiliated fund can take (studio/investor dual role) (attribution approximate) — [SignatureBlock](https://signatureblock.co/articles/venture-studios)

### Inferences
- The criticism is partly being absorbed rather than rebutted: the drop in target ownership from 80-90% to ~25% is the industry conceding the cap-table point.
- Because the pro-studio statistics all trace to one industry body and the critics rely mostly on anecdote, neither side has rigorous data; the honest position is "unproven", not "proven better" or "proven worse".

### Gaps
- No on-record statement by a YC partner (Garry Tan, Michael Seibel, Dalton Caldwell), a16z or First Round specifically criticising venture studios was found; the brief's premise that such statements exist could not be confirmed. Do not attribute the critique to them.
- Adverse selection of founders and weak "ownership mentality" are asserted in opinion pieces but no dataset testing them was found.
- No Carta or PitchBook dataset isolating studio-founded companies (graduation rates, valuations) was found.

## 2. Which studios have shut down, downsized or pivoted, and why?

### Takeaway
Documented North American cases are retrenchments rather than collapses: Redesign Health cut staff twice (2022, 2024), Madrona Venture Labs was folded back into its parent VC (2025), AI Fund is adding a growth fund (2026); the clearest outright failures are corporate studios (Mach49 wound down, BCG Digital Ventures absorbed, Walmart/SAP/IKEA/Telefónica units closed). An unverified industry count says ~154 studios closed in 2024, the first net decline.

### Cited Findings
- Redesign Health (New York, healthcare): laid off 67 employees (~20%) in October 2022, about a month after a $65M Series C, calling it a restructuring — [Becker's](https://beckershospitalreview.com/digital-health/healthcare-startup-launcher-redesign-health-lays-off-67-employees.html); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/redesign-health-lays-67-employees-restructuring-move)
- Redesign Health cut a further 77 positions in 2024 while slowing the pace of new company launches; the CEO said the US venture downturn "will last longer than the company had anticipated and hoped" — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/redesign-health-reduces-workforce-it-slows-down-pace-new-company-launches-2024)
- Madrona Venture Labs (Seattle): absorbed into Madrona Venture Group in 2025 after 10+ years as an independent studio and ~30 incubated startups; seven staff laid off (three weeks' notice, two months' severance); leader Mike Fridgen left to become COO of Rover. Stated rationale: Seattle's ecosystem now has far more incubators and founder resources than when the studio launched. Its last fund was only $11M (fifth fund, June 2023) — [GeekWire 2025](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/return-to-the-mother-ship-seattle-startup-studio-madrona-venture-labs-is-absorbed-by-madrona/); [GeekWire 2023](https://www.geekwire.com/2023/madrona-venture-labs-raises-11m-for-fifth-fund-as-studio-doubles-down-on-ai-startups/)
- Pioneer Square Labs (Seattle): no layoffs or closure found; it has launched 40+ startups and vetted 500+ ideas over a decade and has encoded its playbook into "Lev", an AI co-founder product — a pivot in method, not a retreat — [GeekWire PSL tag](https://www.geekwire.com/tag/psl/)
- AI Fund (Andrew Ng): closed Fund II at ~$190M in May 2025 (Fund I $175M) and by February 2026 had filed to raise a $100M growth fund, a shift from pure studio toward later-stage backing (attribution approximate, aggregator profiles) — [F4 Fund profile](https://f4.fund/firms/ai-fund); [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/investor/ai-fund)
- Rocket Internet (Berlin): announced delisting 1 September 2020, six years after IPO; market value had fallen from as much as EUR 6.7B on IPO day to ~EUR 2.6B; buyback offered at EUR 18.57, below the prior close of EUR 18.95. Stated reason: private growth capital is abundant, so a listing is unnecessary and delisting allows long-term strategy at lower cost — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/09/01/startup-factory-rocket-internet-to-delist.html); [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2020/09/01/rocket-internet-delist/)
- eFounders → Hexa (Paris): rebranded November 2022 into a holding company of several studios (eFounders for SaaS, Logic Founders for fintech, 3founders for web3), explicitly to become less dependent on its founders and scale toward ~30 companies a year; AI is now one of its verticals — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/29/efounders-morphs-into-hexa-a-portfolio-company-of-startup-studios); [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2022/11/30/efounders-rebrands-to-hexa/); [Hexa Letter #2](https://www.hexa.com/blog/news/hexa-letter-2-the-path-from-1-to-30-companies-a-year)
- BCG Digital Ventures: grouped with BCG Gamma and Platinion under the BCG X brand in September 2022 (unit of ~3,000 launched December 2022); the standalone corporate-venture-builder identity was absorbed — [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/32263/bcg-groups-gamma-platinion-and-ventures-under-one-roof-bcg-x); [Consultancy.com.au](https://www.consultancy.com.au/news/6229/bcg-launches-3000-strong-tech-build-design-unit-bcg-x)
- Mach49 (Silicon Valley corporate venture builder, acquired by Next 15 in 2020): in August 2025 Next 15 said it would shut it down after finding possible serious misconduct, dismissed three senior managers and referred the matter to law enforcement; earn-out is in arbitration with a counterclaim; an "orderly wind down" is expected to finish before the end of FY26 — [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/corporate-venturer/next-15-shutting-mach49-misconduct-allegations/?format=md); [BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/?p=176457)
- Corporate studios: a vendor report on five failed corporate studios (including units tied to Walmart, SAP and IKEA) locates the failures in the parent relationship — returns too slow for corporate stakeholders, shifting priorities; Telefónica wound down its Alpha programme (which incubated Koa Health) as priorities changed (practitioner/vendor view) — [Bundl: 5 failed venture studios](https://www.bundl.com/reports/5-failed-venture-studios); [Global Venturing: CVC units closed 2024](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/cvc-units-closed-2024/?format=md)
- Industry-wide count: ~154 studios closed in 2024, the first year the global total declined, leaving 1,100+ active; characterised as "a correction rather than a collapse". Single secondary source, underlying dataset not named — unverified — [0100 Conferences newsletter](https://0100conferences.substack.com/p/rethinking-venture-building-de-risking)
- A paywalled LP newsletter titled "Venture studio closures are rising" cites a 2024 study of 3,452 deals, 1,107 studios and 861 outreach responses; its closure figures could not be read — [LP Blueprint](https://metz.substack.com/p/venture-studio-closures-are-risingwhat)

### Inferences
- The recurring failure pattern for independent studios is fixed overhead funded on the assumption of a steady launch cadence and easy follow-on capital; when seed/Series A markets tightened (2022-2024) the cadence slowed and staff were cut (Redesign Health is the textbook case).
- For VC-affiliated studios the pattern is absorption: once the parent's ecosystem matures or AI lowers build cost, a separately staffed studio with a tiny fund ($11M) is hard to justify.
- Corporate studios fail for a different reason — parent governance and patience — not venture-market conditions.

### Gaps
- No reliable sources were found in this session on Human Ventures, Kairos, Juxtapose, Science Inc (post-SPAC), Expa, Betaworks, Obvious, Atomic changes, or "Quibi-era" studio failures. Their status as of 2026 is unverified here; do not state they closed or downsized.
- No source found on BCG Digital Ventures layoffs specifically, or on Mach49 headcount reductions before the wind-down.
- No source found on a Redesign Health pivot after 2024.
- Documented post-mortems written by the studios themselves were not found.

## 3. What are the documented success factors?

### Takeaway
Sources converge on five factors: moderate studio ownership (~25%) with real capital behind it, sector specialisation, aggressive early kill discipline, a genuine distribution or domain advantage rather than build capacity, and a structure that separates the co-founder and investor roles. Evidence is practitioner consensus and one survey, not controlled studies.

### Cited Findings
- Founder-equity fairness / capital alignment: top studios target ~25%; equity taken should scale with capital and services provided — [Highline Beta podcast](https://highlinebeta.com/podcast/the-future-of-venture-studios-liquidity-structure-scaling-with-sarah-anderson-founding-partner-at-vault-fund); [Focused Chaos](https://www.focusedchaos.co/p/good-venture-studio-deal)
- Sector focus: among new venture-studio funds, 65% focus on a single sector vs 15% generalist; leading sectors are AI (28%), software (16%), B2B (13%) (survey of emerging-manager funds run by Decile Group / Founder Institute's VC Lab — an interested party) — [Founder Institute](https://fi.co/insight/the-rise-of-venture-studio-funds-in-emerging-vc); [Decile Group](https://decilegroup.com/articles/venture-studio-funds-emerging-vc)
- Kill discipline: studios are said to kill 80-90% of concepts at validation, by design (no independent benchmark; attribution approximate) — [VCBeast](https://vcbeast.com/the-venture-studio-model)
- Structure: separating the studio's co-founder role from its investor role ("Tulsa Model") to avoid misaligned cap tables — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/byron-dailey-and-gitwit-discuss-their-development-of-a-new-venture-studio-model)
- Studio quality matters measurably: studio-level differences explain ~30% of the variance in venture outcomes, far more than country, industry or vintage effects (peer-reviewed) — [Patel & Chan 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/veecee/v26y2024i3p283-301.html)
- Distribution advantage: commentary argues founders will trade equity for a studio that can deliver real customers at launch (e.g. "10,000 qualified users"), and that a LinkedIn following is not distribution (opinion, studio-industry blog) — [StartupStudios.com](https://startupstudios.com/post/the-startup-studio-reckoning-why-2026-will-kill-the-idea-factory-model)
- Life-science studios are presented as a sub-segment where the model performs well (trade-press claim, September 2025) — [BioBuzz](https://news.biobuzz.io/2025/09/07/quietly-dominating-why-life-science-venture-studios-are-outpacing-traditional-vc/)
- Liquidity and fund structure (how the studio itself is financed and how LPs get paid) are flagged as the open design problem for scaling — [Highline Beta podcast](https://highlinebeta.com/podcast/the-future-of-venture-studios-liquidity-structure-scaling-with-sarah-anderson-founding-partner-at-vault-fund)

### Inferences
- The success factors are the mirror image of the criticisms in section 1; a studio's stake, capital contribution and sector depth are the three things an outside investor or founder can check quickly.
- "Repeatable playbook" is being productised (PSL's Lev), which suggests playbooks are becoming less proprietary.

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence linking any single factor (e.g. equity level) to outcomes.
- Specific current terms for High Alpha and Atomic were not found.

## 4. How have the 2022-2025 downturn and the 2025-2026 AI boom affected studios?

### Takeaway
The downturn forced launch-cadence cuts and layoffs at capital-heavy studios and produced the first reported net decline in studio count (2024), yet studio formation among emerging managers accelerated in 2025, overwhelmingly AI-themed. Capital is polarising: small AI studio funds ($6M-$190M) at one end and billion-dollar VC "creation" and roll-up vehicles at the other.

### Cited Findings
- Venture-studio funds rose from 6% of new emerging-VC funds in 2024 to 13% in 2025 (Decile Group / VC Lab cohort data; interested party) — [Decile Group](https://decilegroup.com/articles/venture-studio-funds-emerging-vc); [Founder Institute](https://fi.co/insight/the-rise-of-venture-studio-funds-in-emerging-vc)
- Redesign Health's 2024 cuts were explicitly tied to a venture downturn lasting longer than expected — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/redesign-health-reduces-workforce-it-slows-down-pace-new-company-launches-2024)
- General seed-to-Series A conditions (all startups, not studio-specific): the 2022 seed cohort had a 15.4% two-year graduation rate vs 30.6% for the 2018 cohort (attribution approximate); Carta shows only ~6-9% of late-2022/2023 seed companies raised a Series A within a year, a median seed-to-A interval of 616 days in Q2 2025, and the 2024 seed cohort graduating faster than 2023 (December 2025) — [Carta: still an uphill battle](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-seed-to-series-a-still-uphill-battle/); [Carta: graduating faster](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-seed-to-series-a-graduation-improving-2024/); [StealthAgents compilation](https://stealthagents.com/research/startup-fundraising-statistics-2026)
- Venture-backed shutdowns overall are at a record in 2026, per a16z data cited by Foley (October 2026) — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/10/record-startup-shutdowns-reflect-a-market-that-is-moving-forward/)
- New / recent studio capital: AI Fund II ~$190M (May 2025); Forum Ventures $20M studio fund to co-build 30 AI-native B2B companies in North America; super{set} +$90M for data/AI companies; Inevitable AI Group $6M pre-seed led by Aleph (paid press release); Merantix Capital EUR 103M (June 2026), split equally between studio companies and direct investments; OSS Ventures (Paris/Boston) EUR 75M fund with EUR 44M first close; NovaWave Capital (LG) launched WaveX AI studio with the Arizona Commerce Authority — [F4 Fund](https://f4.fund/firms/ai-fund); [Forum Ventures](https://www.forumvc.com/thought-pieces/introducing-our-venture-studio-a-new-way-to-build-ai-startups); [TechCrunch on super{set}](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2683747); [TechCrunch press release](https://techcrunch.com/press-release/inevitable-ai-group-raises-6m-from-aleph-to-launch-ai-native-saas-companies/); [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/06/04/merantix-capital-launches-eur103m-fund-for-early-stage-european-ai-startups/); [Enhance Monitor](https://enhance.online/blog/2026-02-28-enhance-monitor-8); [Techli](https://techli.com/?p=10780)
- Family offices are reported to be using studios to build companies directly (Crain Currency) — [Crain Currency](https://www.craincurrency.com/family-office-management/family-offices-quietly-using-venture-studios-build-companies)

### Inferences
- LP appetite appears bifurcated: institutional money flows to large-platform "creation" strategies (section 5), while independent studios raise small funds from emerging-manager LPs, family offices and public/regional bodies.
- Studio companies face the same Series A bar as everyone else; the claimed studio advantage in graduation rates is not confirmed by neutral data for 2022-2025 vintages.

### Gaps
- No studio-specific graduation, valuation or fund-size series from Carta or PitchBook was found; no PitchBook analyst note on studios located.
- No direct LP survey on appetite for studio vehicles found.
- Atomic's most recent fund size and date not verified in this session.

## 5. How is AI changing the model?

### Takeaway
AI is collapsing the cost of building, which removes the studio's traditional selling point (shared engineering) and shifts its defensible value to distribution, domain access and capital. The largest "studio-like" activity in North America has moved into VC-run AI roll-ups and incubations (General Catalyst, Thrive Holdings, Long Lake), whose margin claims remain self-reported.

### Cited Findings
**Value proposition shift**
- Andy Budd (17 March 2026) argues AI may improve studio economics and predicts the "one-person venture studio" — a founder-operator using AI, contractors and a little capital — [Andy Budd](https://andybudd.com/archives/2026/03/will-ai-super-charge-venture-studios)
- Cheaper prototypes shift studio value "toward distribution and operating leverage rather than raw engineering throughput" (2026 teardown of founder discussions) — [Discury](https://discury.io/digest/teardown-venture-studio-service-fees-and-value-proposition-d253)
- A studio-industry blog predicts 2026 will "kill the idea factory model", arguing studios that treat AI as a productivity hack will lose to those rebuilt around AI-native execution (opinion) — [StartupStudios.com](https://startupstudios.com/post/the-startup-studio-reckoning-why-2026-will-kill-the-idea-factory-model)
- Counterpoint: vibe coding "compresses execution, not judgment, not distribution"; validation did not get faster (developer commentary) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/lmoncany/why-vibe-coding-is-making-the-customer-problem-worse-not-better-5290)
- McKinsey reports AI is materially compressing venture-building timelines and raising productivity (consultancy with a venture-building practice) — [McKinsey PDF](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/business%20building/our%20insights/how%20to%20build%20businesses%20faster%20and%20better%20with%20ai/how-to-build-businesses-faster-and-better-with-ai_final.pdf)
- Claims of 30-day builds by "AI-native" studios come from a paid press release in which the issuer ranks itself first — discount heavily — [TFSF press release](https://lifestyle.capitalcityrock.com/story/143942/tfsf-ventures-ranks-best-ai-venture-studios-2026-separating-builders-from-advisors/)
- Playbook-as-software: PSL's "Lev" AI co-founder — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/tag/psl/)
- Forum Ventures defines AI-native as a product designed around AI from day zero — [Forum Ventures](https://www.forumvc.com/thought-pieces/what-an-ai-native-b2b-company-actually-means)

**AI roll-ups / services-as-software (VC firms doing studio-style creation)**
- General Catalyst's "creation strategy": ~$1.5B earmarked, roughly ten to twelve co-created roll-up vehicles in legal, IT managed services, customer service etc.; goal of at least doubling acquired companies' EBITDA margin; GC says it commits $100M+ per platform and holds long term (figures conflict across sources: one cites $750M) — [TechCrunch, 28 Sept 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/28/the-ai-services-transformation-may-be-harder-than-vcs-think/); [Capital & Clarity](https://capitalandclarity.substack.com/p/the-general-catalyst-behind-15-billion); [PE Insights](https://peinsights.substack.com/p/thrive-and-general-catalyst-embrace)
- TechCrunch's headline judgement (September 2025): the AI services transformation "may be harder than VCs think" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/28/the-ai-services-transformation-may-be-harder-than-vcs-think/)
- Thrive Holdings: $1B+ vehicle for AI-driven services businesses (launch date reported as 2024 or April 2025); OpenAI researchers embed with its engineers and OpenAI took an equity stake in December 2025; reported $12B valuation in August 2026 per a tracker; group revenue/EBITDA and AI's profit contribution are undisclosed; backed Shield (IT services, $100M) and Crete/Current (nearly 50 accounting firms, $300M+ revenue reported) — [PE Insights](https://peinsights.substack.com/p/thrive-and-general-catalyst-embrace); [Caritas Ventures market map](https://caritas.ventures/ai-rollup-market-map/); [AI Rollup Tracker](https://izdebska.com/tracker)
- Slow Ventures and 8VC are also running the buy-legacy-businesses playbook; Slow funded several roll-ups with little upfront capital and was early in Metropolis (parking) (Newcomer, June 2025, partly paywalled) — [Newcomer: Inside the VC roll-up craze](https://www.newcomer.co/p/inside-the-vc-roll-up-craze-that); [Newcomer: deals accelerate](https://www.newcomer.co/p/ai-roll-up-deals-accelerate-as-top)
- Scale by mid-2026: Long Lake agreed in May 2026 to take American Express Global Business Travel private for $6.3B at a 65% premium, the category's largest public-market deal — [CNBC, 8 June 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/08/silicon-valleys-new-buyout-playbook-is-hitting-wall-street.html)
- Crescendo (GC-backed) reports 60-65% gross margins, about four times the industry norm — reported, not audited — [Caritas: Do AI rollups work?](https://caritas.ventures/ai-rollup-market-map/do-ai-rollups-work/)
- Bear case: the best names are the most visible, failures are under-reported (Renovo Home Partners collapsed), and AI margin numbers are "early, mostly self-reported, and produced by the people raising money on them" — [Caritas](https://caritas.ventures/ai-rollup-market-map/do-ai-rollups-work/); [Linear #186](https://www.newsletter.lukesophinos.com/p/linear-186-ai-roll-ups-two-years)

### Inferences
- The competitive threat to independent North American studios is less "founders can build alone" and more "multi-stage VCs now incubate and acquire with billions"; studios without distribution or capital are squeezed from both sides.
- The roll-up model repeats the studio sector's evidentiary weakness: headline metrics are published by the sponsors.
- The same AI that lowers studio cost also lowers the founder's need for a studio, so net effect on studio economics is ambiguous; sources disagree.

### Gaps
- Nothing verifiable found on Atomic's AI companies, Audacious, or named "agentic"/solo-founder studios beyond opinion pieces.
- No audited financials for any AI roll-up platform; no independent measure of studio build-cost reduction.
- Hexa's AI focus is only confirmed as one vertical among several; no detail on scale.

## 6. What do industry reports say about market size, number of studios and forecasts?

### Takeaway
No credible dollar market-size report or numeric 2026+ forecast for venture studios exists in what was found; the only quantities are studio counts (roughly 1,000-1,100 globally in 2024-2026) that come from industry or secondary sources with differing methods. Forecasts are qualitative: consolidation, AI specialisation, lower equity stakes.

- Counts: 800+ studios worldwide as of July 2023; ~877 by early 2023, roughly double five years earlier; 1,100+ active after ~154 closures in 2024; a 2024 study dataset covers 1,107 studios and 3,452 deals (sources and methods differ) — [0100 Conferences](https://0100conferences.substack.com/p/rethinking-venture-building-de-risking); [LP Blueprint](https://metz.substack.com/p/venture-studio-closures-are-risingwhat); [GoingVC](https://www.goingvc.com/post/the-rise-of-venture-studios)
