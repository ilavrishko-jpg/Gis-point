# Startup Studio Operating Model and Playbook (North America, as of 2026)

> Method note for the report writer: in this session page fetching was blocked for most hosts (psl.com, geekwire.com, flagshippioneering.com, medium.com, andybudd.com all failed to resolve). Every finding below therefore comes from web-search result summaries of the linked pages, not from a full read of the page. Numbers are reproduced as the search results reported them. Where a figure is company-reported, single-source, or from a vendor/aggregator, it is flagged. Nothing here was verified against the full primary text.

## 1. Stages of the studio funnel, stage gates, timelines, budgets, kill rates

### Takeaway
Studios converge on a five-step sequence (ideation → validation → creation/MVP → spinout → scale) with tranche-based funding released at gates, and the dominant pattern is very heavy attrition at validation: roughly 90% of ideas killed at PSL, about 99% at FutureSight, and 80-100 explorations yielding 6-8 NewCos per year at Flagship. Fixed, comparable per-stage budgets and timelines are not published by most studios; the available figures are single-firm examples.

### Cited Findings
- Most studios use similar stage gates, moving from ideation through validation, traction and creation to spin-out, with each studio adapting the recipe; another framing is ideation, validation, creation, spinning out, scaling up — [Founders Factory](https://foundersfactory.substack.com/p/venture-studios-everything-you-need?open=false); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/resources/from-ideation-to-execution-a-step-by-step-guide-to-nurturing-startups-in-a-venture-studio)
- PSL uses a five-stage process: ideation, validation, creation, spin out, scale up; "most ideas are killed by the end of validation" — [Innovation Leader profile of PSL](https://www.innovationleader.com/uncategorized/venture-studio-profile-pioneer-square-labs/)
- PSL funnel over its first five years: 25 spinouts, "the other 225 were killed" (about 10% of tested ideas spun out, about 5 spinouts per year); almost all 25 spinouts became venture-backed — [GeekWire 2021](https://www.geekwire.com/2021/seattle-startup-studio-pioneer-square-labs-spun-25-ideas-5-years-way/)
- PSL year one: 22 ideas axed / 18 projects killed (figures differ between headline and body as reported by search) and 3 companies spun out — [GeekWire 2016](https://www.geekwire.com/2016/startup-studio-quickly-axed-22-ideas-spun-three-companies-one-year/)
- Flagship Pioneering funnel: 80 to 100 "explorations" a year → numbered prototype companies "ProtoCos" (e.g., FL1, FL63) → "NewCos" (get a name, board, CEO and significant Flagship capital) → "GrowthCos" (spun out with outside investors and partners); Flagship forms six to eight NewCos a year — [Flagship Pioneering: process](https://www.flagshippioneering.com/process)
- Third-party figures for Flagship: 50-100 "what if" questions per year; validated prototypes receive roughly $25M+ each financed internally; another aggregator lists $5-50M+ founding rounds and a 4-8 week NewCo founding decision window (unverified aggregator claims) — [F4 Fund profile](https://f4.fund/firms/flagship-pioneering)
- FutureSight (San Francisco / Toronto, vertical B2B AI software): validation is meant to "kill as many ideas as we can" (co-founder John Carbrey); only about one percent of concepts survive the first stage; once a venture reaches formation, a 24-week process provides pre-seed capital, recruits initial customers and launches an MVP (company-reported) — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/futuresight-turns-company-building-into-a-science/)
- High Alpha's sequence: Exploration (deep dives into markets, problems, trends, competitors, technologies) → Design (the four-day Sprint Week) → Launch (founder recruited; studio supplies marketing, finance, design; MVP, early customers, leadership hires, investors) — [High Alpha: New Ventures team approach](https://www.highalpha.com/blog/new-ventures-team-approach-to-scaling-businesses); [High Alpha: Sprint Week](https://highalpha.com/sprint-week-a-forcing-function-for-innovation)
- Budget tranching example (European studio, Builders): about a 10-week period with roughly €25k spent before gate releases of roughly €150k — [Builders Studio: The math behind our startup studio](https://medium.com/builders-studio/the-math-behind-our-startup-studio-cfa4f4b857ff)
- Atomic: initial investment reported at $300K-$500K "to prove out the thesis and build an MVP" (aggregator figure; other listings say $500K-$3M or $1M-$3M); TechCrunch put Atomic's own checks at typically $200,000 to $2 million — [F4 Fund: Atomic](https://f4.fund/firms/atomic); [TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/03/02/atomic-which-only-funds-the-startups-it-launches-just-closed-its-newest-fund-with-260-million/)
- Hexa (Paris, builds B2B SaaS/AI companies sold globally including the US): invests €700,000 through a capital increase for 33.33% equity; founders are on board for 12 months while product and fundraising are built together (deal page last updated 16 Dec 2025) — [Hexa: the deal](https://www.hexa.com/start/deal); [Hexa: why build with Hexa](https://www.hexa.com/build-companies-with-hexa)
- Forum Ventures AI Studio (North America): a 5-week, part-time sprint to pressure-test an idea, then $250K invested at formation/incorporation; founders keep majority equity and board control — [Forum: The Foundry](https://www.forumvc.com/builders-between-ventures); [Forum AI Studio](https://www.forumvc.com/ai-studio)
- AI Fund: a builder takes a studio-sourced idea through a full-time, on-site 12-week sprint; successful company formation can bring $500,000 to $1 million of pre-seed investment; if formation does not happen, IP stays with AI Fund (single third-party profile, may be outdated) — [Chopping Block profile of AI Fund](https://www.choppingblock.ai/companies/aifund); program page: [AI Fund: Build with us](https://aifund.ai/build-with-us-corporate)
- Corporate/partner-studio variant: after validation, ventures go to a second investment committee to decide on spinout and additional capital, then join a six-month accelerator programme — [Highline Beta](https://www.highlinebeta.com/blog/designing-a-corporate-venture-studio-that-succeeds)
- GSSN-attributed outcomes: 84% of studio startups raise a seed round; 72% reach Series A vs 42% for traditional ventures; founding to Series A in 25 months vs 56 months; 53% return vs 21.3% — [Bundl summary of GSSN data](https://www.bundl.com/articles/why-venture-studio-startups-have-higher-long-term-success-rates); [High Alpha](https://highalpha.com/why-venture-studios-are-the-future-of-company-building/). These are from a studio-promoting network with a sample of a few dozen member studios, cited inconsistently by year and label across secondary sources — [Politecnico di Torino thesis](https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/16456/1/tesi.pdf)
- Independent-ish benchmark: median 2.32 years from foundation to seed for acquired studio startups (individual's spreadsheet using older data) — [Google Sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1ogcZCZLL0o4fvtJOPluhq1ygroZknTYdRh94oHPIJwU/edit)
- Allocator guidance: studios should document their kill rate and its reasoning, and define validation framework, decision rights, budget/hiring control, pivot approval and shutdown triggers — [Altss taxonomy: venture studio](https://altss.com/taxonomy/venture-studio); [VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/venture-studio-model-how-it-works-when-makes-sense)

### Inferences
- A composite North American software-studio funnel consistent with the cited data: tens to hundreds of ideas screened per year → about 10% (PSL) down to about 1% (FutureSight) survive validation → 3-5 spinouts per year for a typical studio. The "100 ideas → 10 validated → 2-4 spinouts" shape in the brief is consistent with PSL's 250 → 25 record, but no source states that exact three-step ratio.
- Capital committed pre-spinout is small (tens of thousands per idea in validation, as in the Builders example); the first real check arrives at formation and clusters at $250K-$1M for software studios (Forum $250K, Atomic about $300-500K, AI Fund $500K-$1M, Hexa €700K). Biotech (Flagship) is an order of magnitude or two larger.
- Time from formal start to MVP/pre-seed is typically 3-6 months in the published programs (AI Fund 12 weeks, FutureSight 24 weeks, Forum 5-week sprint before formation); Hexa's 12-month residency is the long end.

### Gaps
- No studio in the sample publishes a full gate-by-gate table with timeline, budget and pass rate for every stage. Per-stage kill percentages between validation and spinout (e.g., MVP stage failures) were not found.
- The GSSN reports and Big Venture Studio Research (Max Pog) reports could not be retrieved directly; the GSSN numbers above are secondhand.
- "Startup Studio Playbook" (Szigeti) and "Venture Studios Demystified" were not accessed; no claims from them are included.

## 2. How specific studios run it

### Takeaway
The named studios split into three operating archetypes: idea-first studios that generate and validate internally and then recruit a CEO (Atomic, PSL, High Alpha, Hexa, FutureSight, AI Fund, Redesign Health, Flagship, Idealab), founder-first residency programs that select people and let ideas form around them (Antler, Human Ventures), and hybrids (Forum, Atomic "starting either with a founder or an idea").

### Cited Findings
- **Atomic** (Miami; founded 2012 by Jack Abraham): co-founds companies and only writes checks to startups it spins up itself; does not take pitches or inbound; reported 800+ ideas in its database; launched 14 startups in the 12 months to Nov 2021; launches "starting either with a founder or an idea"; an Atomic Business Services team handles back-end functions from recruiting to accounting; portfolio includes Hims & Hers, Bungalow, Found, Homebound, OpenStore, Replicant; fund III $260M (2021), fund IV reported at $320M — [TechCrunch Nov 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/17/talking-atomic/); [TechCrunch Mar 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/03/02/atomic-which-only-funds-the-startups-it-launches-just-closed-its-newest-fund-with-260-million/); [Harbus](https://archive.harbus.org/2019/meet-atomic-silicon-valleys-coolest-vc-that-is-not-actually-a-vc/); [Refresh Miami](https://refreshmiami.com/news/miami-based-venture-studio-atomic-raises-320m-for-its-4th-fund/); [F4 Fund](https://f4.fund/firms/atomic)
- **High Alpha** (Indianapolis; B2B SaaS; studio + VC fund since 2015): Sprint Week is a four-day, team-based event; one account reports 60+ people in teams shaping 5 concepts over 4 days; each partner sits on a different team; teams produce a brand and pricing model; partners choose which concept to fund; "all" studio companies are born from Sprint Week; the studio builds around five companies at any time; typical output about three per year, a record 10 in 2020, 30 companies from 2015 to early 2021; a later source says 90+ early-stage SaaS companies created and invested in — [High Alpha: Sprint Week](https://highalpha.com/sprint-week-a-forcing-function-for-innovation); [Medium: Sprint Week in 4 days](https://medium.com/high-alpha/sprint-week-what-to-expect-when-creating-a-company-in-4-days-d6b940e150bf); [High Alpha Studio III](https://highalpha.com/high-alpha-studio-iii/); [High Alpha: venture studio](https://www.highalpha.com/venture-studio); [High Alpha capital](https://highalpha.com/capital/)
- High Alpha Innovation (corporate-partner arm): equity is split between High Alpha and the sponsoring corporation after founder and employee allocations — [TechCrunch 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/05/21/indianapolis-venture-studio-high-alpha-launches-new-business-bringing-studio-model-to-corporations)
- **Pioneer Square Labs** (Seattle): raised $12.5M in 2015 to "dream up, then kill off or spin out startups"; 250 ideas tested → 25 spinouts in five years; uses "test-driven learning" to find "product-market pull"; has reduced the upfront equity it takes and adjusts by founder experience; declines to publish its stake; now on Studio III; 2026 spinout Lev is an "AI co-founder for startups" led by PSL's T.A. McCann — [TechCrunch 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/10/01/psl/amp/); [GeekWire 2021](https://www.geekwire.com/2021/seattle-startup-studio-pioneer-square-labs-spun-25-ideas-5-years-way/); [PSL: test-driven learning](https://www.psl.com/feed-posts/5-steps-to-embrace-test-driven-learning-to-find-product-market-pull-pmp); [PSL Studio III](https://www.psl.com/feed-posts/introducing-pioneer-square-labs-studio-iii-why-now-part-i-of-ii); [GeekWire 2026](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/psls-t-a-mccann-is-running-a-startup-again-as-the-ceo-of-lev-an-ai-co-founder-for-startups/)
- **Hexa / eFounders**: recruits two co-founders (CEO and CTO) per project and sets up a dedicated initial product team; core team in product, marketing, design and recruitment works hands-on; 50+ companies launched with $4.5B+ combined valuation; older eFounders structure was 25% as third co-founder plus 25% as investor; current deal €700K for 33.33%; modeled outcome after seed is founders about 50-53% and Hexa 25-27%; partner page says Hexa and partner keep 30% fully diluted after the startup leaves; 2026 letter renames company-building "Start" and adds "Scale" and "Sprint" programs; stated ambitions of 30 companies a year and 100+ companies by 2030 — [Hexa deal](https://www.hexa.com/start/deal); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexa_(company)); [Hexa: become a partner](https://hexa.com/become-a-partner); [Hexa Letter #2](https://www.hexa.com/blog/news/hexa-letter-2-the-path-from-1-to-30-companies-a-year); [Hexa financial strategy](https://www.hexa.com/blog/news/hexas-financial-strategy-to-launch-100-companies-by-2030); [Medium: studio financing](https://medium.com/@qnickmans/c6763c826ac0)
- **Idealab** (Pasadena; Bill Gross; since 1996): 150+ companies with 7 billion-dollar exits in one account, 175+ in a later one; method is to "test an idea as quickly, and as cheaply, as possible to see if people want it," then find a CEO; if nobody internally wants to pursue an idea it is treated as a weak idea; in 2026 launched Idealab.ai publishing 1.2 million AI-generated business ideas — [Diamandis](https://www.diamandis.com/blog/9-key-lessons-derived-from-150-startups); [Ross Dawson](https://rossdawson.com/startup-studios/bill-gross-idealab-company-builder-successful); [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/bill-gross-launches-idealabai-gives-away-12m-startup-ideas)
- **Redesign Health** (New York; healthcare; founded 2018 by Brett Shaheen): co-founds, staffs and funds healthcare companies, typically as founding/lead investor; "venture chairs" lead the CEO and co-founder search; raised a $175M fund in Dec 2024; stated goal of 100 companies; Saudi JV with Sanabil targets at least 20 companies — [Giant Health](https://www.giant.health/blog/536/inside-redesign-healths-bid-to-change-the-healthca); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241212128956/en/); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/how-venture-firm-redesign-health-charting-its-future-amid-us-and-global-growth); [F4 Fund](https://f4.fund/firms/redesign-health)
- **AI Fund** (Andrew Ng; founded 2017): studio teams research and validate a problem (idea, product, market, funding validation) and then hire a Founder in Residence who takes the venture from concept to company; roles on the studio side include Venture Advisors, Principal/Product/Technical Builders; summaries of Ng's talks say AI Fund averages about one startup per month and treats execution speed as a key predictor of success — [AI Fund FIR posting](https://jobs.lever.co/AIFund/c8041983-b650-4863-a7e5-282956eb671e); [AI Fund Venture Advisor posting](https://jobs.lever.co/AIFund/05f0785e-1447-4ccf-875e-100ec6589a78); [Deciphr summary of "Building Faster with AI"](https://www.deciphr.ai/podcast/andrew-ng-building-faster-with-ai)
- **Flagship Pioneering** (Cambridge MA; biotech): described in HBS case 718-484 "Institutionalized Entrepreneurship: Flagship Pioneering"; ProtoCos that cannot validate in the lab are discontinued; Pioneering Medicines is the in-house drug unit leading the Pfizer partnership — [Gary Pisano case page](https://gpisano.com/case-study/institutionalized-entrepreneurship-flagship-pioneering/); [Flagship process](https://www.flagshippioneering.com/process); [Flagship: Pioneering Medicines](https://www.flagshippioneering.com/pioneering-medicines)
- **Antler** (residency model; US hubs New York, Austin, San Francisco; a US-S26 cohort dated 23 March 2026): in-person residency of six to eight weeks depending on source and year; founders pitch the US Investment Committee at the end; investment is not guaranteed; no fee; 2023 US terms were $250K on a SAFE at $2.75M post-money (about 9.09%); a later summary claims $500K-$1M initial commitment (unverified) — [Antler residency](https://www.antler.co/residency); [Antler: revamped US terms](https://www.antler.co/blog/antler-reveals-revamped-us-investment-terms); [StartupResearcher](https://www.startupresearcher.com/opportunity-radar/antler-us-residency)
- **FutureSight**: founders are matched with pre-validated ideas rather than pitching their own; about 1,000 founder inquiries a month with two selected; nearly all are repeat founders with exits; portfolio company CrewScope raised a $1M pre-seed in 2025; runs monthly public briefings ("6 ventures we're building in August 2025", "4 ventures we're building in November 2025") — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/futuresight-turns-company-building-into-a-science/); [Superscout](https://superscout.co/venture-studio/futuresight); [Luma Aug 2025](https://luma.com/4hh0ojuc); [Luma Nov 2025](https://luma.com/q5afcoc2)
- **Human Ventures** (New York; Heather Hartnett and Joe Marchese): founder-first; an entrepreneur-in-residence cohort program where founders bring their own ideas and run them through a structured validation process; selection weighs personality traits, skills and lived experience; the firm later deliberately slowed company building to focus more on seed-fund investing — [Human Ventures: Dissecting the Venture Studio Model](https://medium.com/human-ventures/dissecting-the-venture-studio-model-22f9b6792cef); [Venture Studio Insider](https://medium.com/venture-studio-insider/inside-human-ventures-with-heather-hartnett-be548713f63b); [The Gallery interview with Joe Marchese](https://thegallery.beehiiv.com/p/human-ventures-joe-marchese-journey-building-studio-raising-capital-navigating-attention-economy)

### Inferences
- The live monthly cadence FutureSight advertises (4-6 ventures in build at once) and High Alpha's "around five at any time" suggest that a single software studio team carries roughly 4-6 concurrent ventures in build.
- Human Ventures' retreat toward seed investing and Hexa's 2026 restructuring into partner-led programs indicate studios adjust the model toward lighter-touch or platform formats when in-house building does not scale.

### Gaps
- Redesign Health's stage definitions, CEO equity and annual launch count were not found. High Alpha's check size and founder split are unpublished. PSL's stake is undisclosed.
- AI Fund's full official stage list was not found; the 12-week / $500K-$1M terms come from one third-party profile.
- Enhance Ventures, Mach49, Rule 1 Ventures, Venture Studio Forum and Studio Hub material was not reached.
- Current (2026) Antler US terms could not be confirmed.

## 3. Validating demand for the US market; go/kill metrics

### Takeaway
Named studios describe validation qualitatively (test cheaply, kill fast, design partners before code) but almost none publish numeric go/kill thresholds; the numeric thresholds available come from general landing-page-testing guides, not from studios.

### Cited Findings
- PSL frames validation as "test-driven learning" aimed at "product-market pull", with most ideas dying by the end of validation — [PSL](https://www.psl.com/feed-posts/5-steps-to-embrace-test-driven-learning-to-find-product-market-pull-pmp); [Innovation Leader](https://www.innovationleader.com/uncategorized/venture-studio-profile-pioneer-square-labs/)
- Idealab's question: how to test an idea as quickly and cheaply as possible to see if people want it — [Diamandis](https://www.diamandis.com/blog/9-key-lessons-derived-from-150-startups)
- Forum Ventures AI Studio offers introductions to a network of 300+ Fortune 500 / Fortune 1000 companies for product feedback, design partners and first customers (third-party listing of Forum's offer) — [F4 Fund: Forum Ventures](https://f4.fund/firms/forum-ventures); [Forum AI Studio](https://www.forumvc.com/ai-studio)
- FutureSight's 24-week post-formation program includes recruiting initial customers alongside the MVP — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/futuresight-turns-company-building-into-a-science/)
- AI Fund validates problem definition with market analysis and early customer validation before hiring the FIR — [AI Fund Venture Advisor posting](https://jobs.lever.co/AIFund/05f0785e-1447-4ccf-875e-100ec6589a78)
- Andrew Ng: "Working on concrete ideas lets you go faster" because a concrete idea can be quickly validated or falsified — [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/andrew-ng-generative-ai-shifts-startup-bottleneck-to-product-management)
- Generic smoke-test benchmarks (not studio-specific): set the win threshold before launch; roughly 1-3% conversion from paid traffic on a simulated purchase click is a good signal — [Kromatic Real Startup Book: landing page smoke test](https://kromatic.com/real-startup-book/2-evaluative-market-experiments/value-proposition-test/landing-page-smoke-test/)
- B2B Google Ads conversion averages 2-5%; sub-1% is an early warning of poor message-market fit (vendor blog citing WordStream) — [LeadEnforce](https://leadenforce.com/blog/how-to-test-new-markets-with-minimal-ad-spend)
- A go/no-go call after about 500 visitors weighing conversion rate, pricing clicks, survey responses and organic growth; at least 200-300 unique visitors per variant before reading results (vendor rules of thumb) — [Validea guide](https://validea.pages.dev/resources/guides/landing-page-conversion-validation); [HTMLPub](https://htmlpub.com/blog/most-landing-page-ab-tests-never-reach-significance)

### Inferences
- For B2B (the dominant North American studio category: High Alpha, FutureSight, Forum, Hexa), design partners and early customer commitments appear to be the decisive gate evidence, with landing-page/ads tests more relevant to consumer and SMB concepts (PSL, Atomic, Idealab style).
- Since thresholds are unpublished, a report should present numeric cutoffs as practitioner rules of thumb rather than studio standards.

### Gaps
- No studio-published numeric criteria were found for: number of customer discovery interviews per idea, number of LOIs/design partners required to pass a gate, pre-sale revenue thresholds, or ad-test budgets. This is the weakest-sourced area of the brief.
- No US-specific validation nuance (versus other markets) was found in sources.

## 4. Sourcing and recruiting founders/CEOs; compensation and equity

### Takeaway
Idea-first studios recruit a CEO/co-founder after internal validation (via EIR/FIR roles or direct search), while residency models select founders first; studio ownership at formation ranges widely, with about a third being the most frequently documented figure and a visible trend toward studios taking less to keep cap tables fundable.

### Cited Findings
- Industry survey cited by GeekWire: studios take on average a 34% equity stake at founding, ranging from about 15% to upwards of 80% — [GeekWire 2021](https://www.geekwire.com/2021/seattle-startup-studio-pioneer-square-labs-spun-25-ideas-5-years-way/)
- Other ranges reported: studios "often take 30-60% upfront"; 10%-80% depending on model; one source claims most take 15-20% — [VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/venture-studio-model-how-it-works-when-makes-sense); [OpenVC](https://openvc.app/blog/startup-studios)
- Hexa: 33.33% for €700K; founders about 50-53% after seed — [Hexa deal](https://www.hexa.com/start/deal)
- PSL has reduced its upfront stake and varies it with the founder's entrepreneurial experience — [GeekWire 2021](https://www.geekwire.com/2021/seattle-startup-studio-pioneer-square-labs-spun-25-ideas-5-years-way/)
- Atomic: recruits a co-founder for its ideas, "will still make them a co-founder" and wants "to give them a huge amount of equity"; split varies with the value the person adds — [TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/17/talking-atomic/)
- Forum Ventures: founders keep majority equity and board control — [Forum: The Foundry](https://www.forumvc.com/builders-between-ventures)
- Antler US (2023 terms): $250K for about 9% via SAFE, decided after the residency — [Antler](https://www.antler.co/blog/antler-reveals-revamped-us-investment-terms)
- FutureSight recruits "Co-Founder & CEO – Entrepreneur in Residence" roles per venture and matches repeat founders to pre-validated ideas; 2 selected from about 1,000 monthly inquiries — [FutureSight job posting](https://apply.workable.com/futuresight/jobs/view/8611543DCB.md); [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/futuresight-turns-company-building-into-a-science/)
- Redesign Health: venture chairs lead the search for the CEO and co-founders; playbook described as recruiting experienced operators who want to be a startup CEO with equity upside (blogger's summary) — [Giant Health](https://www.giant.health/blog/536/inside-redesign-healths-bid-to-change-the-healthca); [Shawn Dimantha Substack](https://shawndimantha.substack.com/p/venture-studio-model-in-digital-health)
- High Alpha recruits a founder candidate during Exploration, before Sprint Week; first co-founder-led Sprint Week is documented — [High Alpha](https://highalpha.com/high-alphas-first-co-founder-led-sprint-week)
- Flagship NewCos get a CEO and board at the NewCo stage, after ProtoCo validation — [Flagship process](https://www.flagshippioneering.com/process)
- EIR pay is inconsistent across postings: one studio-linked listing shows $150K base with 0.5% equity participation; another is a contractor role at $2,500 for up to six months; one studio states a 70% target ownership for the founding team; another sets equity, salary and structure "at the point of commitment" — [Cache posting](https://apply.workable.com/cache/jobs/view/E48B1D285C); [Variance Ventures EIR](https://venturecapitalcareers.com/companies/variance-ventures/jobs/entrepreneur-in-residence-eir); [Wilbur Labs EIR](https://jobs.lever.co/wilburlabs/5a0696fb-33e8-49cc-856b-8a20fa2245a3) (attribution of individual figures to specific postings is approximate, taken from a search summary)
- Cap-table friction: some Series A/B investors are skeptical of heavily studio-owned cap tables; "dead equity" remains after the studio's operational involvement ends; some studios add management or success fees; one founder reported being asked for 60% for $100K total funding — [The Venture Studio Trap](https://tremendous.blog/2023/07/14/the-venture-studio-trap); [Alder VC](https://aldervc.pages.dev/blog/venture-studio-equity/); [Discury teardown](https://digest.discury.io/teardown-venture-studio-equity-demands-3942)
- Fenwick "Tulsa Model" (with Gitwit): separates the studio's two roles, co-founder and investor, to align incentives, improve tax efficiency and keep the cap table clear for investors; Fenwick also publishes formation and initial capitalization considerations for studios — [Fenwick: Byron Dailey and Gitwit](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/byron-dailey-and-gitwit-discuss-their-development-of-a-new-venture-studio-model); [Fenwick: formation considerations](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/venture-studios-startup-formation-initial-capitalization-considerations)
- AI Fund Venture Advisor role is equity-only — [AI Fund posting](https://jobs.lever.co/AIFund/05f0785e-1447-4ccf-875e-100ec6589a78)

### Inferences
- Documented North American terms are moving toward founder-majority structures (Forum, PSL's reduction, Atomic's "huge amount of equity"), likely in response to downstream investor resistance to large studio stakes.
- Equity taken scales with how much the studio contributes: about 9% for a residency check (Antler), about a third for idea + team + capital (Hexa, survey average), more for studios that originate everything and hire a CEO late.

### Gaps
- No reliable benchmark was found for EIR/FIR salary during residency at the major studios, founder vesting terms, or what happens to a founder's equity if the idea is killed.
- Individual studio stakes for Atomic, High Alpha, PSL, Redesign Health, AI Fund and FutureSight are not public in the sources reached.

## 5. Core studio team and shared services

### Takeaway
The studio operating company employs a shared team of partners plus product, design, engineering, recruiting, marketing and finance/legal specialists who are spread across 3-5 ventures; sources describe the functions consistently but give almost no hard headcount numbers or post-spinout service durations.

### Cited Findings
- The operating company employs the shared team (engineers, designers, recruiters, finance) and burns cash building new companies; legal formation, cap table work, recruiting and finance operations are day-one infrastructure from shared services — [VC Beast: the venture studio model](https://vcbeast.com/the-venture-studio-model)
- Shared specialists can cover a 3-4 startup pipeline; dedicated pods per venture push costs up quickly — [Highline Beta: how to staff a venture studio](https://www.highlinebeta.com/blog/how-to-properly-staff-a-venture-studio)
- Headcount is driven by output: building 1 venture a year needs fewer people than 2-4+; many studios target 3-5 startups a year — [Focused Chaos: how to build a venture studio team](https://www.focusedchaos.co/p/how-to-build-a-venture-studio-team)
- High Alpha: a "squad" of experts (marketing, finance, design and others) and a New Ventures team work with founders; Sprint Week draws 60+ participants — [High Alpha: Meet the Squad](https://www.highalpha.com/blog/designed-experts-for-first-time-and-seasoned-founders); [High Alpha Sprint Week](https://highalpha.com/sprint-week-a-forcing-function-for-innovation)
- Atomic: built-in team of experts; Atomic Business Services covers back-end functions from recruiting to accounting — [Harbus](https://archive.harbus.org/2019/meet-atomic-silicon-valleys-coolest-vc-that-is-not-actually-a-vc/); [Atomic blog](https://www.atomic.vc/blog-posts/additions-to-your-co-founding-team)
- Hexa: core team in product, marketing, design and recruitment, hands-on with founders for the 12-month build period — [Hexa](https://www.hexa.com/build-companies-with-hexa)
- AI Fund staff roles: Venture Advisors, Principal Builders, Product Builders, Technical Builders, visiting engineers — [AI Fund Principal Builder posting](https://jobs.lever.co/AIFund/b4498e9d-bc69-4da2-b88f-26968879adc2); [AI Fund: build with us](https://aifund.ai/build-with-us-corporate)
- Redesign Health: venture chairs plus an advisory network ("Redesign Health Advise") meant to shorten time from idea to funding — [Redesign Health](https://www.redesignhealth.com/insights/redesign-health-advise-leveraging-experts-and-insights-to-expand-impact)

### Inferences
- Explicit post-spinout service windows found: Hexa 12 months; FutureSight 24 weeks from formation; Highline Beta-style six-month accelerator after spinout. Elsewhere support tapers as the spinout hires its own team, typically around the seed round.

### Gaps
- No sourced headcount for any named studio's core team; no sourced ratio of studio staff to ventures; no data on whether shared services are charged back to spinouts in North American studios (fees are mentioned only as a criticism in section 4).

## 6. Companies launched per year and what determines throughput

### Takeaway
A typical studio launches about 3-5 companies a year; well-capitalized outliers reach 10-14, and throughput is governed by the validated-idea pipeline, availability of qualified founders/CEOs, shared-team capacity and capital.

### Cited Findings
- Many studios target 3-5 startups per year — [Focused Chaos](https://www.focusedchaos.co/p/how-to-build-a-venture-studio-team)
- PSL: 25 spinouts in five years (about 5 per year); 3 in year one — [GeekWire 2021](https://www.geekwire.com/2021/seattle-startup-studio-pioneer-square-labs-spun-25-ideas-5-years-way/); [GeekWire 2016](https://www.geekwire.com/2016/startup-studio-quickly-axed-22-ideas-spun-three-companies-one-year/)
- High Alpha: typical three per year, record 10 in 2020 — [High Alpha Studio III](https://highalpha.com/high-alpha-studio-iii/)
- Atomic: 14 in 12 months (2021) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/17/talking-atomic/)
- AI Fund: about one per month (summary of Ng's talk) — [Deciphr](https://www.deciphr.ai/podcast/andrew-ng-building-faster-with-ai)
- Flagship: 6-8 NewCos per year from 80-100 explorations — [Flagship](https://www.flagshippioneering.com/process)
- Forum Ventures: plans to co-build 30 AI-native businesses across North America with a $20M studio fund — [Forum](https://www.forumvc.com/thought-pieces/forum-ventures-raises-20m-ai-venture-studio)
- Hexa: ambition to go from 1 to 30 companies a year — [Hexa Letter #2](https://www.hexa.com/blog/news/hexa-letter-2-the-path-from-1-to-30-companies-a-year)
- Corporate studio counter-example (Arup): perhaps one candidate every 12-18 months, too few to justify a permanent venture-building factory — [Global Venturing case studies](https://globalventuring.com/intelligence/?p=4616)
- 2024 landscape analysis counted 1,100+ studios founded worldwide; LP newsletter reports venture studio closures rising — [GoingVC](https://www.goingvc.com/post/the-rise-of-venture-studios); [LP Blueprint](https://metz.substack.com/p/venture-studio-closures-are-risingwhat)

### Inferences
- Forum's plan implies about $667K of fund capital per company ($20M / 30), consistent with a $250K formation check plus reserves and studio costs.
- Studios raising throughput above about 5 per year do so by either large funds (Atomic $260-320M) or by shifting work to founders and partners (Hexa's partner platform, residency models).

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 annual launch counts were found for High Alpha, PSL, Atomic or Redesign Health. Atomic's 14-per-year figure is from 2021 and may not reflect current pace.

## 7. AI tools and agentic development: effect on studio speed and cost, 2024-2026

### Takeaway
Credible sources agree AI coding tools are compressing prototype/MVP time and shifting the bottleneck from engineering to product decisions and customer feedback, and new AI-native studios are organized around small teams and domain-expert founders; the specific speed-up multiples in circulation come mainly from vendors and are unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Andrew Ng (AI Fund): AI-assisted coding has made engineering fast enough that product management, deciding what to build and getting user feedback, is now the bottleneck; a Stanford CS230 fall 2025 lecture slide says the engineer:PM ratio is trending down, even to 1:1 — [AOL / Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/andrew-ng-says-real-bottleneck-051543175.html); [Stanford CS230 lecture 8](https://cs230.stanford.edu/syllabus/fall_2025/8/lecture_8.pdf)
- McKinsey: widespread AI use appears to be materially compressing venture-building timelines and raising productivity, letting teams generate, test and validate more ideas faster — [McKinsey: how to build businesses faster and better with AI](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/business%20building/our%20insights/how%20to%20build%20businesses%20faster%20and%20better%20with%20ai/how-to-build-businesses-faster-and-better-with-ai_final.pdf)
- Andy Budd (March 2026): the next generation of venture studios will likely run lean, with smaller teams, faster cycles, less infrastructure and fewer people needed to reach first proof — [Andy Budd](https://andybudd.com/archives/2026/03/will-ai-super-charge-venture-studios)
- FutureSight framing: "It's never been easier to build, but it's never been harder to win" — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/futuresight-turns-company-building-into-a-science/)
- Forum Ventures launched an AI-first studio for AI-native B2B companies built with domain-expert founders from day zero, backed by a $20M fund — [Forum](https://www.forumvc.com/thought-pieces/introducing-our-venture-studio-a-new-way-to-build-ai-startups)
- PSL: "best way to do AI is kill bad ideas early"; its 2026 spinout Lev builds an "AI co-founder for startups" — [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/information-technology/cvc-unplugged-best-way-to-do-ai-is-kill-bad-ideas-early-pioneer-square-labs); [GeekWire 2026](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/lev-the-psl-spinout-building-an-ai-co-founder-for-startups-names-tech-vet-jay-bartot-as-cto/)
- Idealab.ai (2026) publishes 1.2 million AI-generated business ideas drawn from 17 million sources, free to use, i.e., idea generation is being commoditized — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/bill-gross-launches-idealabai-gives-away-12m-startup-ideas)
- Vendor claims (unverified): time-to-MVP cut from 6-9 months to 2-3 months; fastest AI-native builders at 30 days; MVPs in under two days — [Codiste](https://www.codiste.com/ai-reduces-cost-time-for-venture-studio-startups); [TFSF Ventures press release](https://lifestyle.capitalcityrock.com/story/143942/tfsf-ventures-ranks-best-ai-venture-studios-2026-separating-builders-from-advisors/); [TrendHunter on LeanAI Ventures](https://www.trendhunter.com/trends/leanai-ventures)
- Counterpoints: demo speed is cheap, while authorization, data boundaries, deployment and eval loops for LLM features are where projects stall; a Hacker News commenter argues prototyping was never the real bottleneck; one industry blog argues 2026 will kill the "idea factory" studio model — [Hashnode](https://hashnode.com/posts/can-we-use-ai-for-mvp-development-in-2026-a-practical-research-backed-perspective/6a4b5ea284381b1bb724c4fb); [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44521225); [StartupStudios.com](https://startupstudios.com/post/the-startup-studio-reckoning-why-2026-will-kill-the-idea-factory-model)

### Inferences
- If building and idea generation are both cheap, studio differentiation moves to what AI does not supply: validated demand (design partners, distribution), founder selection and capital. This matches FutureSight's validation-heavy model and Forum's customer-network pitch.
- Cheaper MVPs weaken the case for large studio equity stakes justified by engineering labor, reinforcing the founder-majority trend in section 4. No source states this directly.

### Gaps
- No studio in the sample published before/after figures for build cost, team size per venture or time-to-MVP attributable to AI tooling. Quantified claims are vendor marketing.
- The Andy Budd and McKinsey pieces could not be read in full; only their summarized conclusions are reported.
