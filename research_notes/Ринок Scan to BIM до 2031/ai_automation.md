## 1. Which startups and tools do automated scan-to-BIM today; funding 2023-2026; claims vs. independent evaluation

### Takeaway
There is a long tail of tools, but no evidence of a venture-scale winner in pure scan-to-BIM automation: the funding rounds found are small (USD 3-10M seed/Series A) and every automation percentage found (70%, 75%, 90%, 95%) is a vendor or blog claim with no published methodology. Independent academic comparison describes commercial tools as semi-automatic: semantic classification plus assisted fitting, or a black-box service that returns a low-LOD model which is then refined by hand.

### Inferences
- Funding found for pure scan-to-BIM automation in 2023-2026 is one to two orders of magnitude smaller than for construction progress monitoring (see section 4: Buildots alone USD 175M in 2025-2026). Capital is going to QA/progress analytics and capture hardware, not to "auto-modelling" software. This is consistent with Foundamental's (an investor's) public scepticism.
- Scanner OEMs (Leica/Hexagon, Topcon/ClearEdge3D, Trimble, XGRIDS, NavLive) are embedding classification and basic element extraction into their own stacks. For a service firm this means the "easy" architectural shell is the part most likely to be commoditised or bundled with hardware, and in XGRIDS' case locked to the vendor's capture format.
- No tool was found with an independently verified automation percentage. All percentages should be treated as upper-bound marketing figures on favourable (orthogonal, uncluttered, office-like) scenes.

## 2. State of research: 3D segmentation foundation models, scan-to-BIM benchmarks, LLM/agentic and text-to-BIM; accuracy by discipline

### Takeaway
Semantic segmentation of indoor point clouds is strong for common classes (about 80% mIoU on standard benchmarks) and self-supervised "foundation" encoders arrived in 2025-2026, but turning segments into correct, connected, parametric BIM is still a research problem: published pipelines cover walls/slabs/openings in regular buildings, MEP best cases are ~90% of pipes and ~80% of ducts within 10 mm with weak results on small pipes and rectangular ducts, and LLM agents editing IFC score about 50% on the newest benchmarks. No published work was found that goes from point cloud to BIM with an LLM agent end to end.

### Cited Findings

Segmentation / foundation models
- [PEER, CVPR 2025] Sonata (self-supervised, Point Transformer V3 encoder, trained on 140k scenes): linear-probe ScanNet semantic segmentation rises from 21.8% to 72.5% mIoU, above DINOv2 image features lifted to 3D (63.1%); supervised PTv3 from scratch is 77.6% val mIoU on ScanNet — [CVPR 2025 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Wu_Sonata_Self-Supervised_Learning_of_Reliable_Point_Representations_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)
- [Aggregator, verify against paper] Sonata + PTv3 fine-tuned: 79.4 mIoU ScanNet val, 36.8 on ScanNet200 (200 classes), 82.3 S3DIS 6-fold, 76.0 S3DIS Area 5 — [HyperAI listing](https://vercel.hyper.ai/en/papers/2503.16429)
- [PEER, Mar 2026] PointINS (instance-aware self-supervised learning, PTv3 backbone): linear probing 79.0 mIoU ScanNet and 73.6 S3DIS Area 5; authors say about 80-90% of supervised performance — [arXiv 2603.25165](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25165)

Scan-to-BIM reconstruction (architectural/structural)
- [PEER] An entry in the 2nd International Scan-to-BIM Challenge (CV4AEC) that placed 2nd in 3D reconstruction reported F-scores of only 0.316 at 5 cm, 0.454 at 10 cm and 0.584 at 20 cm tolerance — [ISPRS Archives 2024](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLVIII-2-W8-2024/403/2024/isprs-archives-XLVIII-2-W8-2024-403-2024.pdf)
- [PEER, Apr 2026 preprint / EC3 2026] BIMStruct3D (DFKI), a "fully automated hybrid learning" pipeline with topology refinement: strong on CV4AEC office scenes with standard wall/door layouts, outperforms a RANSAC baseline (Kaufmann et al. 2022) on 3D-IoU and volumetric IoU at 5 cm voxels; authors state dedicated door detection is still needed — [arXiv 2604.24311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24311); [EC3 2026](https://ec-3.org/publication/ec32026_197/)
- [PEER, Mar 2025] Cloud2BIM: open-source point cloud to IFC for slabs, walls, openings and room zones, handles non-orthogonal walls, "up to seven times faster than the fastest competing tool"; struggles to tell windows from doors; IFC opens in Revit, Archicad, FreeCAD, Blender/Bonsai — [arXiv 2503.11498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11498v2)
- [PEER] An Aalto University thesis on automating scan-to-BIM found the process "could not be fully automated, as it required considerable manual intervention to correct errors" — [Aalto](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/da8c6777-d8f8-432f-ac4a-f2dbd94cdff9)

MEP
- [PEER, 2024, Automation in Construction] Omni-Scan2BIM (vision foundation models for MEP scenes): 89.74% of pipes and 80.31% of ducts reconstructed within a 10 mm tolerance; in one test 19.4 m of 21.3 m of duct was reconstructed; remaining gaps are non-circular ducts and small-radius pipes; cites "limited generalization of existing methods to unseen scenarios" without fine-tuning — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0926580524001201). The same 89.74/80.31 figures also appear under a 2025 Journal of Building Engineering record; it is unclear whether these are the same study — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352710225018984)
- [PEER, 2025-2026] Pipeline as-designed BIM update from scans: "point cloud segmentation directly determines the accuracy of the subsequent reconstruction" — [MDPI Buildings](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/16/7/1295)
- [OPINION, Oct 2025] On connectivity: "if the scan doesn't clearly show a connection, the algorithm will likely miss it"; for a parametric model guessing missing data "can be dangerous" while leaving it incomplete "breaks the BIM's usefulness" — [Foundamental](https://www.foundamental.com/perspectives/scan-to-bim-will-it-ever-be-fully-automated-some-thoughts)

LLM / agentic / text-to-BIM
- [PEER, EC3 2026] BIBIMBAP: text-to-BIM benchmark, 100 create/read/update/delete tasks on IFC; best model reaches 50.2%, with failures concentrated in constraint preservation and spatial reasoning — [EC3 2026](https://ec-3.org/publication/ec32026_271/)
- [PEER, Jun 2026] BIM-Edit: 324 natural-language editing tasks on existing IFC models; authors report "a substantial gap between current LLM capabilities and the requirements of structured engineering design workflows" — [arXiv 2606.20146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20146)
- [PEER, EC3 2026] Self-verification agent framework for text-to-BIM (Specifier + Modifier agents in a closed loop); case study only, agents not yet systematically evaluated — [EC3 2026](https://ec-3.org/publication/ec32026_444/)
- [VENDOR, Apr-Jul 2026] Revit 2027 ships Autodesk Assistant (tech preview) and a built-in MCP server; Autodesk describes the public MCP server tech preview as read-only, with write tools planned for a separate server — [Autodesk blog, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/aec/2026/06/17/revit-public-mcp-server/); [Autodesk blog, 7 Apr 2026](https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/aec/2026/04/07/whats-new-in-revit-2027/). Secondary outlets claim a read-write open-source server with 102 tools (18 Jun 2026), contradicting Autodesk's own description — [gentic.news](https://gentic.news/article/autodesk-open-sources-revit-mcp)
- [PRESS, Sep 2026] AEC Magazine test: geometry edits are blocked in Revit's native AI tools but full write access works via Claude + MCP + pyRevit; commercial AI extensions for Revit are "mostly aimed at framing and refining documentation, not direct modelling" — [AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/bim/giving-ai-actual-write-access-in-revit/)
- [PRESS, Apr 2026] The draft revision of ISO 19650-1 (consultation March 2026) does not mention agents or agentic AI — [AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/ai/agentic-bims-missing-infrastructure/)
- [OPINION] A hands-on review found Autodesk Assistant "currently very limited", with most of a dozen attempted tasks failing (which review page said this was not clear from the search extract) — [Architosh, Apr 2026](https://architosh.com/2026/04/autodesk-revit-2027-big-new-ai-and-graphics-changes/)

### Inferences
- The accuracy hierarchy that emerges from the literature: (1) planar architecture — walls, slabs, simple openings — in orthogonal, office-like buildings: automatable today to a draft level; (2) doors/windows typing, columns, non-standard geometry: partial; (3) MEP: pipes good in favourable conditions, ducts and small-bore/insulated/occluded lines poor, connectivity unreliable; (4) structural steel and LOD 350-400 detailing: I found no recent quantitative results at all.
- Segmentation mIoU near 80% on 13-20 common classes falls to under 40% on 200 classes (ScanNet200). Fine-grained element typing, which is what BIM object classification actually needs, is much weaker than headline numbers suggest.
- The plausible 2027-2031 path to more automation is a combination, not one breakthrough: foundation-model segmentation, plus rule/grammar-based parametric fitting, plus LLM agents with write access to Revit/IFC that execute and self-check modelling steps. The enabling plumbing (MCP write access to Revit) appeared in 2026, but benchmark scores of about 50% on simple IFC edits indicate agents are not yet reliable enough for unsupervised production.

### Gaps
- Official CV4AEC 2025 and 2026 challenge leaderboards were not found; the F-scores above are from one entry in an earlier edition.
- No peer-reviewed 2025-2026 review article quantifying per-discipline accuracy (architectural vs. MEP vs. structural) was located; arXiv and ScienceDirect could not be opened to check tables.
- No published point-cloud-to-BIM LLM/agent system was found; this may exist but did not surface.
- No research figures found for structural steel extraction, heritage (HBIM) automation, or LOD 350-400 output.

## 3. Gaussian splatting / NeRF: adoption in AEC and surveying; replacing or complementing point clouds; implications for deliverables

### Takeaway
Between 2025 and 2026 Gaussian splatting moved from novelty to a standardised, widely supported visualisation layer (glTF extension, Cesium/Bentley, Esri, NavVis, Pix4D, DJI, XGRIDS), but the sources consistently describe it as a complement to LiDAR point clouds, not a replacement for measurement-grade work. The one direct threat to modelling services is splat-native capture bundled with automatic Revit element generation (XGRIDS).

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS] Khronos and OGC, with Niantic Spatial, Cesium (Bentley) and Esri, announced the glTF extension KHR_gaussian_splatting (first reported Sep 2025); release candidate announced early February 2026, with a companion SPZ compression extension — [OGC](https://www.ogc.org/ogc-khronos-and-geospatial-leaders-add-3d-gaussian-splats-to-the-gltf-asset-standard/); [AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/reality-capture-modelling/khronos-announces-gltf-gaussian-splatting-extension/); [CG Channel, 5 Feb 2026](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/02/3d-gaussian-splats-are-being-added-to-the-gltf-standard/)
- [VENDOR, 27 Apr 2026] Cesium introduced 3D Gaussian splats with hierarchical level of detail using 3D Tiles, streaming large splat datasets (also in Cesium for Unreal) — [Cesium blog](https://cesium.com/blog/2026/04/27/3d-gaussian-splats-lod/). Bentley acquired Cesium in September 2024 and publicly argues splats "could change infrastructure" — [Bentley blog](https://www.bentley.com/blog/insights/why-gaussian-splats-could-change-infrastructure/)
- [VENDOR, Aug-autumn 2026] NavVis IVION 12 adds Gaussian splats as a third visualisation mode beside point clouds and panoramas; beta only, open to NavVis MLX customers, scans must be under 15 minutes, and splats do not yet support functions such as crop and download — [radiancefields.com, 19 Aug 2026](https://radiancefields.com/navvis-adds-gaussian-splats-to-ivion-12-as-a-third-visualization-mode); [NavVis docs](https://knowledge.navvis.com/docs/how-to-visualize-in-3d-with-navvis-gaussian-splatting)
- [PRESS, 2025-2026] Other point-cloud software adding splat support: Veesus PointClouds2, NUBIGON Pro 7.5.2, 3DF Zephyr 9.0, SimActive Correlator3D 11, Octave Alto 2026.1 — [radiancefields.com NavVis tag index](https://radiancefields.com/tags/navvis)
- [OPINION, 2026] "The State of Gaussian Splatting in 2026": Esri, Bentley, Pix4D, DJI and others added splat support mostly as an appearance layer alongside point-cloud and mesh pipelines; fastest uptake is "as a rapid capture method for documenting change on construction sites", not as a survey instrument; reported zero US companies explicitly offering professional Gaussian splatting as a named service as of early 2026 — [The Future 3D](https://www.thefuture3d.com/blog/state-of-gaussian-splatting-2026/)
- [VENDOR] 3Dsurvey: Gaussian splatting "currently lacks the required centimeter- or millimeter-level accuracy" for measurement — [3Dsurvey](https://3dsurvey.si/what-is-gaussian-splatting/)
- [OPINION] A practitioner source reports splat geometric accuracy of 7.82 cm in a comparison and concludes it "cannot replace LiDAR for dimensional verification"; "the most reliable workflows in AEC anchor splat reconstructions to LiDAR or SLAM data" (the search extract did not make clear which of the two pages below carried each statement) — [Visual Skies](https://visualskies.com/heritage-and-archaeology-3d-scanning/lidar-3d-gaussian-splatting-aec-lifecycle/); [Alpine Reality Capture](https://www.alpinerealitycapture.com/blog/what-is-gaussian-splatting-aec)
- [VENDOR, May 2025] XGRIDS LCC for Revit turns splat/LiDAR captures into Revit levels, walls, doors, windows; vendor claims 3 cm accuracy — [AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/reality-capture-modelling/ai-tool-transforms-gaussian-splats-into-revit-models-lcc-for-revit/)
- [PRESS] AEC Magazine primer on splats for AEC — [AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/technology/introducing-gaussian-splats-for-aec/)

### Inferences
- Deliverables are widening rather than switching: by 2027-2028 clients are likely to expect a photoreal navigable splat alongside the registered point cloud and the model, because viewers they already use (IVION, Cesium, ArcGIS) show it natively. That adds a deliverable type; it does not remove the need for a measured point cloud where tolerances of 1-2 cm or better are contractual.
- Splats lower the bar for "good-enough" visual documentation, which may reduce demand for low-LOD models commissioned only for visualisation or communication, while leaving engineering-grade BIM untouched.
- A vendor claim of 3 cm (XGRIDS) and a practitioner figure near 8 cm both sit outside typical scan-to-BIM accuracy bands for coordination-grade work (USIBD LOA-style millimetre-to-centimetre bands), supporting "complement" for the forecast horizon unless LiDAR-anchored splats become standard.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed survey-grade accuracy study of splat geometry was found; the 7.82 cm figure is from a practitioner blog with unknown test conditions.
- Leica/Hexagon and Autodesk splat support: nothing surfaced (Leica BLK / HxDR and ReCap/Forma were not confirmed either way). Esri production-release timing in ArcGIS Pro was not confirmed.
- NeRF specifically: no current AEC adoption data found; the sources discuss splats almost exclusively.

## 4. Automated QA: scan-vs-BIM deviation analysis and construction progress monitoring

### Takeaway
This is the best-funded and most consolidated adjacent segment: Buildots raised USD 45M (May 2025) and a reported USD 130M (Sept 2026), OpenSpace bought Disperse (Oct 2025), Track3D raised USD 10M (Sept 2025), and Hexagon has folded Avvir's deviation analysis into its reality-capture stack. These products automate comparison against an existing design BIM; they consume models and do not create as-built models.

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS, 29 May 2025] Buildots: USD 45M Series D led by Qumra Capital with OG Venture Partners, TLV Partners and others; total funding then USD 166M — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/29/buildots-raises-45m-to-help-companies-track-construction-progress/)
- [PRESS, 14 Sep 2026] Buildots: USD 130M round reported, led by O.G., aimed at mega-projects/data-centre build-out and portfolio-scale management; CB Insights still lists the USD 45M round as latest, so the two sources are not yet reconciled — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/buildots-raises-130-million/amp/); [Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/business/article/bkvw3ybkfx); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/buildots/financials)

### Inferences
- Automated deviation checking is the mature, productised half of the problem (compare scan with a known model); automatic model creation from scratch is the immature half. Investor money has followed that distinction.
- For service companies these platforms are both a channel and a competitor: they reduce demand for manually produced deviation reports on new-build projects, but they raise demand for a clean, current design/as-built model to compare against, and none of the sources indicate they generate as-built BIM for existing buildings.
- Data-centre and mega-project build-out is the explicit growth narrative (Buildots, Doxel), suggesting where reality-capture budgets will be concentrated in 2027-2029.

## 5. Robotics and autonomous capture reducing field labour

### Takeaway
Autonomous capture is commercially available (Boston Dynamics Spot with Leica BLK ARC since 2024, Trimble turnkey Spot solutions, drones) and new money is entering drone/SLAM capture, but I found no 2025-2026 independent data on field-labour reduction; the quantified claims are vendor figures.

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS, 25 Sep 2024] Leica BLK ARC became the first certified reality-capture payload for Boston Dynamics Spot, fully integrated so that an Autowalk mission including laser scanning can run without an operator present; scan paths can be planned remotely from plans or BIM — [Geo Week News](https://geoweeknews.com/news/leica-blk-arc-boston-dynamics-spot)
- [VENDOR] Boston Dynamics markets autonomous laser scanning for repeatable site documentation; a "95% reduction in inspection time" figure appears in its marketing, with no independent source — [Boston Dynamics](https://bostondynamics.com/solutions/digital-twin/laser-scanning/)
- [PRESS] Hensel Phelps used Spot carrying a Trimble X7; ENR reported about 2 mm point cloud accuracy for site documentation (single-contractor report) — [ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/49369-updated-robot-dog-speeds-site-inspections); [Trimble](https://constructible.trimble.com/in-the-news/advancing-construction-robotics-turnkey-solution-from-trimble-and-boston-dynamics-now-available-3)

- [VENDOR] Handheld/phone capture pushing scanning to non-specialists: Canvas (iPhone/iPad), NavLive (handheld with instant 2D/3D output), XGRIDS (handheld splat/LiDAR) — [World Construction Network](https://www.worldconstructionnetwork.com/news/canvas-secures-10m-series-a-funding); [Print in the Channel](https://printinthechannel.co.uk/navlive-secures-4m-for-aec-industry-with-ai-powered-scanner/)

### Inferences
- Two separate trends reduce field labour: robots automating repeat capture on active construction sites (relevant to progress monitoring), and cheap handheld/phone devices letting the client's own staff capture existing buildings. The second is the more relevant one for scan-to-BIM service firms: capture becomes a commodity the client can do, shifting value to processing, modelling and QA.
- Client-captured data from consumer or low-end SLAM devices is typically noisier and less complete, which tends to make automated modelling harder, not easier; this partially offsets automation gains.

### Gaps

- Nothing found on Flyability, BLK2FLY adoption numbers, or humanoid/other robot platforms for scanning in 2026.

## 6. Expert/analyst views: share of modelling hours AI can remove by 2028-2031; which tasks stay human

### Takeaway
I found no credible analyst or peer-reviewed forecast of the share of modelling hours AI will remove by 2028-2031. What exists is 2026 practitioner/vendor opinion that AI already does roughly 60-85% of detection and first-pass modelling on favourable buildings while humans keep 20-40% (verification, completion, QA), with a reliability ceiling around LOD 200 to low LOD 300; MEP connectivity, LOD 350+, heritage/irregular geometry and occluded areas remain human.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION/VENDOR blog, 2026] "AI handles 60-80% of the heavy work (detection, classification, initial modelling). The human handles 20-40% (verification, completion, QA/QC)"; scan-to-BIM in 2026 is "a semi-automated process". The search extract did not make clear which page carried this; candidates: [nCircle Tech](https://ncircletech.com/blogs/unleashing-speed-in-scan-to-bim-how-ai-ml-are-transforming-point-cloud-to-bim-workflows-in-2026); [Vocal Media](https://vocal.media/01/how-ai-is-automating-scan-to-bim-in-2026); [ATIS](https://www.atis.cloud/articles/scan-to-bim-workflow-guide)

LOD 350 with MEP coordination is still a human-hours job"; "MEP system modeling from scan data, which requires understanding connectivity, not just geometry, is a harder problem and will take longer"; non-standard structures (arches, vaults, heritage elements) still need human expertise (exact page among the 2026 blogs returned was not identifiable from the extract) — [GeoAI](https://www.geoai.au/scan-to-bim-workflow-explained-from-lidar-point-cloud-to-bim-model/); [Optimar](https://optimarprecon.com/scan-to-bim-process/)

- [OPINION, Oct 2025] Investor view: full automation is "still far"; works only in simple, structured environments; missing/occluded data forces a choice between dangerous guessing and an incomplete model — [Foundamental](https://www.foundamental.com/perspectives/scan-to-bim-will-it-ever-be-fully-automated-some-thoughts)

- [VENDOR/OPINION] Optimistic counter-view without evidence: "fully automated Scan to BIM solutions will become the norm" — [Arrival3D](https://arrival3d.com/our-blog/scan-to-bim-reality-capture/)

### Inferences (my own scenario reasoning; not sourced forecasts — label as such in the report)
- A defensible reading of the evidence for planning purposes: by 2028-2031, for standard commercial/residential interiors at LOD 200-300 architecture, first-pass modelling is likely to be mostly automated, leaving review, correction and completion — i.e. a majority of hours removed for that slice. For MEP at LOD 350-400, industrial plants, heritage and heavily occluded or cluttered sites, the evidence supports a much smaller reduction, with automation acting as assistive tooling (pipe-run fitting, classification) rather than autonomous modelling. I found no source that quantifies either figure for 2028-2031.
- Tasks that the sources consistently leave with humans: QA/verification against the cloud; resolving occluded or ambiguous regions; system connectivity and logic in MEP; non-standard and heritage geometry; client-specific standards (families, naming, LOD specification); and sign-off. Liability/certification as a durable human role is a logical inference — no source found discusses professional liability for AI-generated as-built models, and the ISO 19650-1 draft being silent on agents ([AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/ai/agentic-bims-missing-infrastructure/)) suggests governance is unresolved rather than settled.
- The residual human share is likely to be more senior and less scalable through low-cost labour: review and judgement rather than tracing. That changes the labour-arbitrage economics of outsourcing more than the total demand for models.

### Gaps
- No analyst report (McKinsey, Gartner, Cambashi, BuiltWorlds, etc.) with a 2028-2031 quantified automation forecast for scan-to-BIM was found. The percentage splits above are 2026 vendor/consultancy blog opinions without disclosed data.
- No surveyed data on actual hours per m2 before and after AI adoption at service firms.
- Autodesk University, NXT BLD, Intergeo and Geo Week 2026 conference talks were not reachable through search.

## 7. How outsourcing/service firms are adapting (AI-assisted production, productised deliverables, pricing shifts)

### Takeaway
Service firms are publicly rebranding as "AI-powered" or "AI-assisted" hybrid providers and a new tier of automation-first vendors is quoting transparent per-square-foot prices at or below the bottom of traditional outsourcing rates (about USD 0.05-0.18/sq ft versus USD 0.20-1.20/sq ft for manual or MEP-heavy modelling). All pricing evidence is from vendor price guides; no audited data on margins or actual AI usage in production was found.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR price guide, 2026] One guide puts AI/ML-automated work at about USD 0.05-0.15/sq ft and manual or MEP-heavy work at USD 0.20-0.50+/sq ft (attribution among the guides returned was not certain from the extract; likely nCircle or Arrival3D) — [nCircle Tech](https://ncircletech.com/EN/blogs/unleashing-speed-in-scan-to-bim-how-ai-ml-are-transforming-point-cloud-to-bim-workflows-in-2026); [Arrival3D](https://arrival3d.com/scan-to-bim-cost-guide/)

### Inferences
- Three adaptation patterns are visible: (a) incumbents keep the labour model and add AI as marketing and as internal first-pass tooling; (b) automation-first entrants productise — fixed per-sq-ft price, upload portal, fixed LOD/scope, fast turnaround, human review hidden behind the product; (c) distribution moves into platforms (Cintoo, Autodesk Construction Cloud, Matterport, scanner-vendor clouds), where the model is an add-on order rather than a separately procured service.
- Price pressure is concentrated at LOD 200-300 architectural scope, exactly where automation works; the quoted spread implies automation-first vendors are pricing at roughly a quarter to a half of manual rates. MEP-heavy and high-LOD work retains a premium, consistent with section 2.
- Because several "AI" vendors still disclose human review, part of the low pricing may be low-cost labour plus tooling rather than true automation; the sources do not allow this to be separated.
