# Global Scan-to-BIM / Reality Capture / 3D Laser Scanning Market: Size, Growth and Forecasts to ~2031 (state as of November 2026)

> **Method note for the report writer (read first).** Research date: 19 Nov 2026. In this session direct page fetching was blocked by the network allowlist (geoweeknews.com, marketresearch.com, mordorintelligence.com all failed to resolve), so **every figure below was taken from web-search result summaries/snippets, not from a full reading of the source page**. Figures are quoted as the search engine surfaced them; where I could not be sure which listed URL a number came from, I say so ("attribution uncertain"). Nothing was read behind a paywall. Syndicated market-report publishers (GIR, QYResearch, 360iResearch, TBRC, KSI, SkyQuest, etc.) do not disclose methodology and should be treated as low-reliability, order-of-magnitude indicators. The only hard, audited numbers in these notes are the Hexagon and Trimble company filings and the EU/RICS/SCSI institutional sources.

### Inferences
- **Reliability ranking.** Highest: Hexagon/Trimble filings (audited, but not broken out for reality capture). Middle: Mordor, MarketsandMarkets, Grand View (named, widely cited, consistent internal scopes, but paywalled models; Mordor revised its 3D-scanning CAGR down from 14.98% to 13.11% between editions, and GVR's page has a year-label inconsistency). Lowest: GIR, QYResearch, 360iResearch, TBRC, KSI, SkyQuest, and unattributed press-release pages.
- **Reality check on growth rates.** The two dominant hardware/software vendors are reporting mid-single-digit to ~10% organic growth in the relevant divisions in 2026 (Hexagon I&G +2-4%; Trimble Field Systems +7%, AECO +9-10%, AECO ARR +13%). Analyst CAGRs of 13-15% for 3D scanning and 17-30% for digital twins therefore look optimistic relative to what the leaders actually book; the 10-11% end of the range (GVR 10.1% for 3D scanning, MarketsandMarkets 11.3% for BIM) is the more defensible planning assumption. Software/recurring revenue is growing faster than hardware at both vendors.
- **The dedicated scan-to-BIM number should not be used as a headline.** USD 9.1bn for scan-to-BIM services alone would equal the entire BIM market as sized by MarketsandMarkets/Mordor for 2025, and exceed the whole BIM-services market as sized by QYResearch (USD 2.1bn). If one applies the ~10% scan-to-BIM share cited by the outsourcing-services aggregator to BIM-services totals of USD 2.1-7.3bn, the implied scan-to-BIM modelling market is roughly USD 0.2-0.7bn in 2025 — my own back-of-envelope triangulation, not a published figure. TBRC's digital as-builts figure (USD 2.88bn in 2025, including scanning hardware/software and twin creation) is a plausible upper envelope.
- **Spread summary for the writer:** BIM 2030-31 endpoints range USD 15.4bn (MnM, 2030) to USD 21.4-21.8bn (Mordor/second report, 2031); 3D scanning 2030-31 endpoints range USD 6.8bn (GIA) to USD 8.6bn (Mordor); TLS 2024 base ranges USD 3.3-4.4bn with CAGR 5.7-9.2%; digital twin in construction ranges from ~USD 3bn (building twin) to ~USD 65bn (KSI) for 2025 — a >20x definitional spread.

## 3. Regional dynamics: North America, Europe, Middle East, APAC. Where is demand for outsourced scan-to-BIM modelling coming from?

## 4. Regulatory drivers: BIM mandates, ISO 19650, EPBD digital building logbooks, US federal requirements, USIBD LOA specs

### Takeaway
The regulatory tailwind is real but slower than marketing copy suggests: the EPBD recast transposition deadline (29 May 2026) was missed by all 27 Member States and the Commission opened infringement proceedings on 15 July 2026; Poland still has no statutory BIM mandate as of March 2026; Germany is extending BIM from federal infrastructure toward all public works; the UK operates under the ISO 19650-based Information Management Mandate. In the US the relevant instrument for scan work is the voluntary USIBD LOA specification (v3.1, January 2025), not a federal mandate.

### Cited Findings
- EPBD recast (Directive (EU) 2024/1275): Member States had to notify transposition by 29 May 2026 — [European Commission, EPBD](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/energy-efficiency/energy-performance-buildings-directive_en)
- On 15 July 2026 the Commission sent letters of formal notice to all 27 Member States for failing to fully notify EPBD transposition; they have two months to respond before possible reasoned opinions — [ESG Today](https://esgtoday.com/eu-commission-launches-legal-action-against-all-member-states-for-failing-to-implement-zero-emission-building-law/)
- EPBD Article 12 requires each Member State to introduce a renovation passport scheme by 29 May 2026 (Annex VIII framework); passports must be uploadable to the national energy-performance database and stored in or accessible via the digital building logbook where such logbooks exist — [BUILD UP, renovation passports policy guideline](https://build-up.ec.europa.eu/en/resources-and-tools/publications/development-renovation-passports-policy-guideline-summary)
- Member States must submit final national building renovation plans by 31 December 2026 — [European Commission, EPBD](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/energy-efficiency/energy-performance-buildings-directive_en)
- The retrieved EPBD material does not link BIM or scan data explicitly to logbooks or renovation passports (BIM appears only in a glossary) — [BPIE DRP policy guideline](https://www.bpie.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/DRP-Policy-Guideline-Summary-v5-Compressed.pdf)
- EU procurement law (Directive 2014/24/EU) permits but does not mandate BIM in public works — [MDPI, BIM mandate in Poland review](https://www.mdpi.com/2220-9964/13/10/343)
- UK: BIM Level 2 mandatory on centrally procured public projects since 2016, now evolved into the UK BIM Framework and the Information Management Mandate under ISO 19650 — [BIM Branch, mandates 2026](https://www.bimbranch.com/blog/bim-mandates-worldwide-2026)
- Germany: BIM mandatory for federal infrastructure projects since 2020 (Masterplan BIM) and expanding into building construction; a 2026 piece says Germany is extending the mandate from large infrastructure to all public construction works. Sources disagree on historic thresholds (one cites public projects above EUR 100m since 2017) — [BIM Branch](https://www.bimbranch.com/blog/bim-mandates-worldwide-2026); [BIMobject](https://business.bimobject.com/blog/bim-mandates-which-countries-will-require-them-in-2025/)
- Poland: as of March 2026 there is no formal statutory BIM mandate in public procurement; the 2020 roadmap targeted BIM for public contracts above EUR 10m in 2025 and all public procurement by 2030, but the 2025 milestone passed without legislation; one source expects a formal mandate "within 2-4 years" — [ArchBIM, Poland 2026](https://archbim.cloud/en/blog/bim-mandate-public-procurement-poland); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2220-9964/13/10/343)
- USIBD Level of Accuracy (LOA) Specification v3.1 released 21 January 2025 (free download; v3.0 dated August 2019); five accuracy bands LOA10-LOA50; changes in 3.1 were mainly graphics and clearer language. LOA governs accuracy of captured/represented data, complementing BIMForum LOD, which governs model content — [LiDAR News](https://lidarnews.com/usibd-announces-the-release-of-loa-specification-version-3-1-and-launches-new-website-with-education-center/); [LiDAR News on applying LOA v3.1](https://lidarnews.com/leveraging-the-usibd-level-of-accuracy-loa-v3-1-to-enhance-laser-scanning-project-specifications/)

### Inferences
- For late 2026 the honest framing is "regulation is a medium-term driver with slippage": EPBD-driven renovation passports and logbooks will generate demand for as-existing building data, but national schemes are late and the directive does not require BIM or laser scanning as the data source. Demand will arrive unevenly by country through 2027-2030.
- Mandates increase BIM penetration mainly on new public works; for scan-to-BIM the effect is indirect (clients with BIM-based asset information requirements need models of existing estates).
- In North America LOA/LOD literacy is a commercial qualifier rather than a legal requirement: RFPs increasingly specify LOA bands, so an offshore provider needs to quote and validate against USIBD LOA.

### Gaps
- No source found confirming a US federal mandate tying GSA or other agency work to LOA or to scanning; GSA BIM guidance was not retrieved. Status of US federal BIM requirements in 2026 is unverified.
- Status of EPBD infringement after the two-month reply window (i.e., since mid-September 2026) not found.
- Current legal detail for Germany (exact scope/date of extension to federal buildings) not verified against BMDV/BMWSB primary documents; sources are vendor blogs.
- Middle East mandates (Dubai Municipality, Saudi) and APAC mandates (Singapore, Vietnam, India) not researched in this session.
- buildingSMART / ISO 19650 adoption statistics not found.

## 5. Pricing and commoditization: how have prices per m2 / per hour for scan-to-BIM modelling moved; role of offshore outsourcing; margin pressure

### Takeaway
There is no independent time series of scan-to-BIM prices; all evidence is from 2025-2026 vendor price guides. They show a very wide band — roughly USD 0.15-0.75 per sq ft for standard LOD 200-400 architectural modelling, with an emerging ML-automated tier at USD 0.05-0.15 per sq ft and marketplace freelancers at ~USD 0.05-0.06 per sq ft — which is consistent with commoditization of simple modelling while complex/MEP/high-LOD work holds labour-based or day-rate pricing.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 vendor guide: LOD 200 at USD 0.15-0.25/sq ft; LOD 300 at USD 0.25-0.45/sq ft; overall modelling range USD 0.15-0.75/sq ft by LOD — [THE FUTURE 3D, BIM cost per square foot 2026](https://www.thefuture3d.com/learn/bim-cost-per-square-foot/)
- 2026 guide: LOD 400 at USD 0.60-0.75/sq ft; scanning itself costs USD 5,000-15,000 for a typical 50,000 sq ft building, separate from modelling (attribution between this and the previous source is uncertain) — [The World Mag pricing guide](https://theworldmag.com/?p=21604)
- 2026 vendor guide: ~USD 0.05-0.15/sq ft for ML-automated standard projects versus USD 0.20-0.50+/sq ft for manual or MEP-intensive scopes (attribution uncertain between the two listed guides) — [scantobim.online cost guide](https://www.scantobim.online/blog/scan-to-bim-cost/); [ViBIM cost guide](https://vibimglobal.com/blog/scan-to-bim-cost/)
- US provider guide: range from USD 0.10 to USD 8.00+/sq ft; for complex or active sites "the industry is moving away from unit rates toward day rates (USD 3,200-5,000/day) to control scope creep" — [iScano cost guide 2026](https://iscano.com/real-world-applications-laser-scanning-lidar/scan-to-bim-cost-guide/)
- Another guide: standard BIM modelling USD 0.30-0.90/sq ft; scan-to-BIM modelling USD 0.50 to USD 10+/sq ft depending on detail and complexity — [Strand Co](https://strand-co.com/?p=19041)

- IndiaMART listings: BIM modelling at INR 1,200-2,000 per m2 and around INR 1,000 per hour (scope of these listings is unclear and may include more than modelling) — [IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/impcat/bim-modeling-services.html)
- Hourly rates: India/SE Asia USD 15-25(30)/hr; Eastern Europe USD 25-45/hr; UK/US USD 60-120/hr — [BIMOffis](https://bimoffis.com/bim-outsourcing-cost-guide/); [Virtual Building Studio](https://www.virtualbuildingstudio.com/blog/bim-outsourcing-services-cost-usa/)
- Automation: one source says automation tools have cut repetitive modelling effort by 30-40% in standard building types without removing expert validation; another vendor claims AI segmentation now takes under an hour for what took two technician-days (unverified vendor claim) — [nCircle Tech](https://ncircletech.com/blogs/unleashing-speed-in-scan-to-bim-how-ai-ml-are-transforming-point-cloud-to-bim-workflows-in-2026); [ScanToBIM review, DataDrivenAEC](https://datadrivenaec.com/tools/scantobim)

- Hardware-side margin signal: Trimble Field Systems operating margin fell to 29.5% from 33.4% in Q3 2026 on mix shift toward lower-margin hardware — [Zacks](https://zacks.com/stock/news/3024760/trmb-q3-earnings-meet-estimates-as-aeco-growth-lifts-revenues)

### Inferences
- Unit conversion (mine): USD 0.15-0.75/sq ft is about USD 1.6-8.1 per m2; the ML-automated tier of USD 0.05-0.15/sq ft is about USD 0.5-1.6 per m2; the Fiverr rate is about USD 0.55-0.65 per m2.
- The price ladder now has three tiers: (1) automated/self-serve and freelancer at <USD 0.15/sq ft; (2) offshore manual modelling of standard buildings at ~USD 0.15-0.45/sq ft; (3) complex MEP/industrial/heritage/high-LOD and accountable-accuracy work priced by hour or day. Margin pressure is concentrated in tiers 1-2; a USD 25-45/hr Eastern-European cost base is hard to sustain in tier 2 against USD 15-25/hr teams plus 30-40% automation gains.
- The stated shift from unit rates to day rates on complex sites indicates providers are defending margin by re-pricing risk, not by lowering price.
- The direction of travel (prices falling for standard work) is an inference from cross-sectional 2026 vendor guides; I found no dated before/after price data proving the decline.

### Gaps
- No historical price series (e.g., 2020 vs 2026 USD/m2) and no independent survey of scan-to-BIM pricing or provider margins.
- No European (EUR/m2) price benchmarks found; all guides are US-denominated per square foot.
- No Ukraine-, Poland- or Vietnam-specific scan-to-BIM rates.
- No data on share of scan-to-BIM modelling that is outsourced offshore.
- All price sources are vendors marketing their own services; none is neutral.

## 6. Labor shortage in surveying/AEC as a driver

### Takeaway
The surveying workforce shortage in the main buyer markets is well documented for 2026 (US: about a third of surveyors aged 55+; UK: RICS reports mid-career and entry shortages; Ireland: a 2,200+ surveyor shortfall projected for 2026-2029), which supports demand for faster capture technology and outsourced back-office modelling — but no source directly quantifies how much of reality-capture or scan-to-BIM demand the shortage creates.

### Cited Findings
- US: analysis of pooled American Community Survey data 2020-2024 estimates 32.8% of surveyors are 55 or older versus 27.3% aged 18-34, median age 45; BLS projects ~3,100 surveyor openings per year. The same source argues ageing alone is not a crisis; the issue is replacement timing — [Davron, Oct 2026](https://www.davron.net/?p=153834)
- US: a profession blog (March 2026) says the average licensed professional land surveyor is in the upper 50s in most states (~58) — a different, higher figure than the ACS-based median for all surveyors — [Land Surveyors United](https://blog.landsurveyorsunited.com/2026/03/mapping-retirement-wave-where-us.html)
- US: a Washington State study (July 2025) describes a widening shortage of civil engineers and land surveyors constraining infrastructure delivery — [WA State Civil Engineering Pathways 2025](https://depts.washington.edu/trac/bulkdisk/pdf/WA_State_Civil_Engineering_Pathways_2025_FINAL.pdf)
- UK: RICS "Surveying skills: UK snapshot 2026" reports shortages of mid-career professionals (62%) and early-career entrants (47%) as the top drivers of surveying skills shortages — [RICS](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/reports/Surveying-skills_UK-snapshot-2026.pdf)
- Ireland: SCSI projects a shortage of more than 2,200 surveyors over 2026-2029 — [SCSI Employment Outlook 2026-2029](https://scsi.ie/employment-outlook-for-the-surveying-profession-2026-2029/)
- Industry response: Geo Week News reports a firm (ALS) "betting big on robotics and automation" as workforce pressures mount — [Geo Week News](https://www.geoweeknews.com/?p=41763)

### Inferences
- A shrinking licensed-surveyor pool pushes buyer-market firms to (a) capture faster with SLAM/mobile scanners and (b) keep scarce licensed staff on site and client work while pushing registration, modelling and drafting to lower-cost teams. That is the mechanism by which the shortage benefits an offshore/nearshore scan-to-BIM modeller; it is a reasoned inference, not a measured effect.
- The same shortage is also the commercial justification for AI automation of modelling, which over time competes with outsourced labour.

### Gaps
- No quantified link between labour shortage and reality-capture adoption or outsourcing volumes.
- No EU-wide surveyor shortage statistics (CLGE or national bodies in Germany, Netherlands, Nordics) retrieved.
- No AEC-wide (architect/BIM modeller) shortage data for 2026 retrieved; construction craft-labour shortage figures (e.g., ABC, CITB) were not searched.
