## 1. What leading scan-to-BIM and reality capture firms offer and emphasize (2024-2026), and which service lines they added

### Takeaway
The large US players are consolidating and moving up from "scan + model" to platform + lifecycle data (GPRS SiteMap, Hexagon/Multivista/Voyansi, Acuren-NV5, Woolpert), while Indian majors (Pinnacle, Tejjy) bolt digital twin/FM/CMMS and data-center vertical positioning onto modelling. Pure modelling outsourcers increasingly sell as white-label production partners to scanning firms.

### Inferences
- The three consolidators (GPRS, Hexagon, Woolpert/Acuren-NV5) own field capture + client relationship; they still need elastic back-office modelling capacity. The Voyansi deal shows in-house nearshore capacity is one answer; white-label outsourcing is the other. A Ukrainian firm is a supplier to this tier, not a competitor of it.
- New service lines observed repeatedly: (a) FM/digital-twin data layers on top of geometry, (b) client portals/platform delivery, (c) scan-vs-BIM verification during construction, (d) vertical packaging (data centers, plants, utilities). None of the offshore modellers found sells a named subscription "modelling-as-a-service" product publicly; IPX is the closest (on-demand + subscription tiers).
- Indian majors differentiate by scale and adjacent products (CMMS, AI audit tools), which suggests plain "we model point clouds in Revit" is no longer a differentiator at the top end.

## 2. High-growth niches

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced demand niches in 2025-2026 are data centers (dense MEP, tight tolerances, repeated scanning), utility vegetation/wildfire LiDAR analytics (large recurring contracts), and industrial brownfield as-builts; EPBD renovation passports, HBIM, telecom towers, HD maps/road assets, 3D annotation and Gaussian splats are real but either policy-stage, labour-heavy research-stage, or without reliable demand numbers.

### Inferences
- GIS Point already lists powerline classification and LiDAR classification (see section 6); utility vegetation/powerline analytics is the niche where its existing skills meet the best-evidenced recurring contract demand (annual/biannual refresh, multi-thousand-mile programs). The buyers are primes like NV5/Woolpert and regional aerial firms, not utilities directly.
- Data center work is the scan-to-BIM niche with the clearest premium logic (dense MEP, LOA-specified, repeated captures); it rewards MEP modelling competence rather than architectural modelling.
- Industrial brownfield: academic evidence that owners lack expertise supports an outsourcing route, but plant deliverables (P&ID, isometrics, intelligent 3D in AVEVA/Plant 3D) need different tooling than Revit.
- EPBD passports create policy pull for existing-building data in the EU from mid-2026, but I found no evidence yet that this converts into paid scan-to-BIM volume; passports for housing may be satisfied by far cheaper data than a BIM model.
- 3D annotation and HD-map extraction are adjacent labour-arbitrage services for a point-cloud workforce, but automotive annotation is dominated by specialised BPO/tool vendors and the market figures are unreliable.
- Gaussian splats: early, low-barrier, visualization-grade add-on; plausibly an upsell to existing photogrammetry clients, not a standalone revenue line yet.

- No hard number for scanning-specific spend in data centers.

- [DATA] USIBD released LOA Specification v3.1 in January 2025 (minor update of v3.0 from 2019): five levels LOA10-LOA50, separating Measured Accuracy from Represented Accuracy; adds tolerance/standard-deviation conversion guidance. — [Lidar Magazine](https://lidarmag.com/2025/02/09/usibd-a-new-chapter-for-2025/); [Geo Week News](https://www.geoweeknews.com/?p=29371)
- [OPINION, May 2026] Many scan-to-BIM investments fail after handover because nobody planned model maintenance; lighter ongoing updates often beat repeat full scans. — [VIATechnik](https://www.viatechnik.com/insights/blog/why-scan-to-bim-investments-keep-going-south/)
- [DATA] Low-cost SLAM hardware: XGRIDS released the sub-1 kg Lixel K1 handheld in Feb 2024; marketplace listings show Chinese SLAM units around $2,000-2,500 (listing prices, not a trend series). — [Alibaba listing](https://electronics.alibaba.com/product/3d-scanner-architecture)

### Inferences
- Commoditizing: 2D floor plans, area schedules, LOD 100-200 architectural shells, simple residential/retail interiors — all now offered as automated add-ons by the capture platform itself, priced in tens of dollars or cents per sq ft. A labour-based outsourcer cannot defend margin here except through volume contracts or as QA-on-top-of-AI.
- Holding premium (per vendor price spread and human-in-the-loop evidence): LOD 350-400 MEP, industrial piping, heritage/irregular geometry, and anything with a contractually stated LOA and documented QA (represented-accuracy reports).
- The realistic operating model implied by the 85-95% figure: AI pre-modelling + human completion/verification. Outsourcers that adopt the tools keep the job at lower hours; those that do not get underpriced.
- More cheap scanners in non-expert hands means more low-quality point clouds; registration/cleaning/QA of client-captured data is a plausible service line, but I have no source measuring this.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) benchmark of AI scan-to-BIM accuracy or actual hours saved.
- No time series showing price decline for any deliverable; "commoditization" is inferred from product availability and list prices, not measured.
- No USIBD certification programme for v3.1 was found (older v2.0 individual certification existed in 2017-2019).

## 4. Pricing and business model shifts

### Takeaway
Per-square-foot/m2 pricing remains the public default for simple scope, but vendors report a shift to day-rate or fixed lump sum for complex/active sites, platform players quote instantly online and offer subscriptions, and white-label production for scanning companies is an established channel; I found no evidence of formal scan-to-BIM service-partner programs at NavVis or Leica.

### Inferences
- Three coexisting models: (1) unit price per area for commodity scope; (2) day-rate/lump-sum for complex scope; (3) platform ordering with instant quote and optional subscription. For a remote modeller the analogues are: unit-price rate cards for white-label partners, dedicated-team/retainer (monthly capacity) for repeat clients, and a self-service order portal.
- The Voyansi exit and the IMAGINiT remark both point to captive/acquired offshore teams replacing arm's-length outsourcing at larger firms — a threat (lost accounts) and an exit/partnership option for a mid-size outsourcer.
- Turnaround is being advertised in days (48h to 7 days for small sites); a multi-week default is no longer competitive for simple scope.
- Listing channels that modelling firms demonstrably use: Autodesk Marketplace services listings, ACC integrations, hardware-vendor case studies, Clutch.

### Gaps
- No public rate cards for offshore/white-label modelling (per-hour or per-m2 wholesale rates) were found; Clutch reviews for GIS Point do not disclose project costs.
- No evidence of outcome-based pricing in this sector.
- No terms found for NavVis, Leica, Matterport or Autodesk service-partner programs; Cintoo marketplace not found. These need a direct check of the vendors' partner pages.
- ENGINYRING is a single small vendor blog; the "day rates replacing per-sq-ft" claim is opinion, not survey data.

## 5. What clients say they need

### Takeaway
Survey data show skills shortage (not cost) as the top barrier for AEC firms and near-universal AI tool use, which supports outsourcing demand; RFP guidance from vendors stresses explicit LOD + LOA, a BIM execution plan, named team and sample deliverables, with ISO 19650 process maturity and ISO 27001/SOC 2 as security signals — but nearly all "client need" evidence I found is vendor-authored.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA, secondary summary of Autodesk State of Design & Make "2027" edition published 2026] 56% of leaders cite at least one skills-related barrier vs. 37% citing cost; AI/technology advancement is the top organizational concern, up from 31% (2025) to 40%; only 40% say they are approaching or have achieved AI goals. — [IMAGINiT](https://resources.imaginit.com/elevate-your-people-processes/insights-from-autodesks-2027-state-of-design-make-report)
