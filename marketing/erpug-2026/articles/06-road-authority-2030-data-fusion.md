Listen to enough presentations at a road-data conference and a pattern appears. Each technology is impressive — and
each one, on its own, is incomplete. At ERPUG 2026 several speakers said it directly: **there will be no single
winning technology.** The future belongs to those who combine them.

Here are five predictions for how road authorities will work by 2030.

## 1. Continuous instead of periodic

Today most networks are surveyed every one to three years. By 2030, network condition will be refreshed continuously
from **connected vehicles** (roughness from on-board sensors), **fleet cameras** and **smartphone AI**. High-accuracy
survey vehicles will be used for calibration, engineering detail and certification — not as the only source.

## 2. Weather becomes a road-condition input

Freeze-thaw cycles, heavy rain and heat drive deterioration. Combining condition data with **weather history and
forecasts** will let owners predict where damage will appear — and treat it before it does.

## 3. Subsurface joins the surface

The question "why is this road failing?" is often answered below the asphalt. Ground-penetrating radar and other
subsurface data — "not only the ground, but under the ground" — will become part of standard network assessments,
especially for reconstruction programmes.

## 4. Digital twins replace reports

Georeferenced 3D, 360° imagery and condition layers will live in one model. Maintenance, design, safety and finance
teams will query the same twin instead of commissioning separate surveys.

## 5. AI agents work for engineers

AI agents will answer questions like *"Which 50 km should we resurface next year with this budget?"* or *"Show me every
road where roughness increased fastest since spring"*, draft work orders and run budget scenarios. Crucially, as every
speaker stressed, **the engineer keeps final control** of the decision.

## What road owners can do now

- Insist on **open, georeferenced data** so today's surveys feed tomorrow's twin.
- Build a **tiered monitoring** strategy: frequent low-cost + periodic high-accuracy.
- Start **standards-based procurement** (repeatability, certification, turnaround).
- Pilot **data fusion** on one district before scaling to the network.
