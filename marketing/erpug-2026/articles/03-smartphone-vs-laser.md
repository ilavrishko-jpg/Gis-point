One of the most talked-about sessions at ERPUG 2026 was about road condition analysis with nothing more than a
smartphone on the windscreen. The numbers are impressive: one provider reported around 500 road authorities as
customers and roughly 7.5 million kilometres analysed every year. Download an app, press play, drive — and the AI
maps potholes, cracks, line markings and signs, often the same day.

So should every road owner throw out laser profilers and survey vans? No — and to the presenter's credit, they said so
themselves.

## What smartphone AI is great at

- **Frequency.** Drive every week with a maintenance or waste-collection vehicle and you see change as it happens.
- **Coverage.** City streets, bike lanes, gravel roads — places a high-end vehicle rarely goes.
- **Objectivity.** Compared to manual visual inspection, AI is far more repeatable.
- **Cost.** No specialist crew or equipment.

## Where it stops

- **Positioning.** A typical phone gives ~3 m accuracy at 1 Hz. Fine for "this segment is bad", not for an asset
  register or contractor payment.
- **Measurement.** Video sees a crack; it does not measure rut depth to the millimetre or certify IRI.
- **Engineering decisions.** Design, acceptance of works and structural assessment need calibrated, certified data.

## The two-tier model

The direction the industry is converging on is a **tiered stack**:

| Tier | Tool | Frequency | Used for |
|------|------|-----------|----------|
| Monitoring | Smartphone / fleet AI, connected-vehicle roughness | Weekly–monthly | Early warning, pothole response, trends |
| Engineering | 3D laser profiling, IRI, mobile lidar, GNSS/INS | Every 1–3 years | Maintenance programmes, design, acceptance, budgets |
| Decision | GIS platform fusing both + weather + history | Continuous | Prioritisation, scenarios, reporting |

The monitoring tier tells you **where** to look. The engineering tier tells you **what to do** and **how much it
costs**. The decision layer makes sure both live in one place.
