Every road owner has had the experience: two surveys of the same road, two different answers. Which one do you base a
multi-million maintenance programme on?

At ERPUG 2026 in Europe, the answer from the industry was clear — **prove it, with standards**.

## Lesson 1: The US model — certify the equipment and the people

One team described certifying its IRI measurement systems at the National Center for Asphalt Technology (NCAT)
together with a US state Department of Transportation. Test sections covered dense-graded asphalt and open-graded
friction courses, judged against **AASHTO R 56 and R 57** — the American standards for certifying inertial profilers
and operating them.

The striking part was the format: arrive at 9 a.m., capture, process and deliver results the **same day**. Roughly ten
hours. No "we'll send the report next month". The data either meets the standard on the day, or it doesn't.

## Lesson 2: The UK model — approved technology lists

In England, the Department for Transport faced a familiar problem: road condition reported by local authorities was
inconsistent, and new technologies were appearing faster than anyone could evaluate them. The response was
**PAS 2161** — a national test of accuracy and repeatability on selected networks, open to vendors of any technology.
Those who pass go onto an approved list; local authorities choose from it.

The effect is powerful: it creates demand for good technology, and it forces vendors to meet a public bar instead of
marketing claims.

## Lesson 3: Repeatability beats opinion

A vendor presented a 40 km test in which two AI-equipped vehicles and two engineers doing traditional visual
inspection surveyed the same road. The AI results were far more repeatable than the human ones. Whatever technology you
buy, **ask for repeat-run statistics**. If a vendor can't show them, that is your answer.

## What this means for road owners in our region

Standards-driven procurement is spreading. Owners who adopt it early will:

- compare vendors on evidence instead of price alone;
- defend budget decisions to ministries, auditors and international financiers;
- build trend data that is actually comparable year to year.

Practical checklist for your next tender:

1. Require IRI measurement aligned with recognised standards (ASTM E950 / AASHTO R 56–R 57 / relevant EN norms).
2. Require a **repeatability test** on a reference section before the main survey.
3. Specify **turnaround time** and data formats in the contract.
4. Require positioning accuracy suitable for your asset register, not just "GPS".
