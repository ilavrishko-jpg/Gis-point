# Survey Once, Decide Many Times: The New Budget Logic for Road Data

At ERPUG 2026, one sentence from the stage summed up the mood of the whole room: *when budgets are tight, a system
that measures more than one thing with the same investment is simply better use of public money — and everyone wins.*

It sounds obvious. Yet in most countries, road data is still bought in silos. The pavement department commissions
an IRI and rutting survey. The asset team pays for a sign and marking inventory. The bridge unit sends its own crew to
measure clearances. Design consultants re-survey for as-built checks. The same road is driven four or five times, by
four or five contractors, and the results sit in four or five incompatible files.

## The road is the same. The questions are different.

The technology on display at ERPUG 2026 makes the silo model hard to defend:

- **3D laser profilers** capture the whole pavement surface at millimetre resolution — enough for cracks, ruts,
  ravelling and texture in one pass.
- **Mobile laser scanners** add the road corridor: guardrail heights, bridge clearances, drainage, road furniture,
  as-built versus design.
- **360° imagery** lets anyone in the organisation "walk" the road from their desk.
- **Subsurface sensors** extend the picture below the asphalt.
- **AI** turns all of it into located, classified objects — with an engineer still signing off.

One speaker noted that a national standard process captures around 1,800 transverse measurements per scan line —
and then throws most of them away, keeping 32. The data to answer many more questions is often *already collected*.
It just isn't used.

## What "multi-purpose" really means

A multi-purpose survey is not about bolting more sensors onto a van. It's about designing the deliverable around
the *decisions* the owner has to make:

1. **Maintenance planning** — condition indices, IRI, ruts, cracks.
2. **Asset management** — inventory and condition of signs, markings, barriers.
3. **Safety** — clearances, barrier heights, sight lines.
4. **Construction quality** — as-built vs. design, acceptance of contractor works.
5. **Strategic budgeting** — what the network will look like in five years under each funding scenario.

When one survey feeds all five, the cost per answer drops dramatically — and the answers are consistent, because they
come from the same, georeferenced dataset.

## Positioning is the glue

None of this works without accurate positioning. Some national processes still accept ±10 m. Modern GNSS/INS gives
centimetre-level accuracy in open sky. That difference decides whether your data is a set of reports or a **digital
twin** that every department can trust and reuse.
