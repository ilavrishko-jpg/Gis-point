# Beyond the Road Surface: How Condition Surveys Become Road Digital Twins

**Headline options**

---

Many national road condition processes were designed decades ago, around the sensors and computers of the time.
They work. But as one ERPUG 2026 speaker put it: *it isn't a bad process — small changes can improve it a lot.*

Their upgrade path is worth sharing, because it applies to almost every road owner.

## Upgrade 1: Proper positioning

Many standards still accept positional tolerances of around 10 metres. Modern GNSS combined with an inertial
measurement unit (INS) delivers **centimetre-level** accuracy in open sky and controlled drift in tunnels. Multiple
survey runs can be aligned to each other using the 3D data itself.

Why it matters: with good positioning, every crack, sign and barrier becomes a **real object in a global coordinate
system** — reusable in GIS, BIM, asset registers and future surveys. Without it, you have a report.

## Upgrade 2: Panoramic imagery

Forward-facing cameras only see the lane. A **360° camera** captures the whole corridor — and everyone in the
organisation already knows how to use it, because they use street-level imagery every day. Analysts can check objects
beyond the carriageway without a site visit.

## Upgrade 3: 3D mobile laser scanning

Adding a mobile lidar (millions of points per second) is the step with the biggest impact. Suddenly the same survey
supports:

- **as-built vs. design** comparison;
- **bridge and overpass clearance**;
- **guardrail height** and road-restraint compliance;
- **drainage** and kerb inspection;
- volume and geometry measurements of anything in the corridor.

The data becomes versatile — "not only focused on road condition".

## Upgrade 4: AI + 3D = located objects

AI on images is now standard. Combine it with depth or point-cloud data and every detection gets a precise real-world
position. Keep a **human in the loop** for quality control — that remains essential.

## Don't throw data away

One more lesson: transverse profile scanners often capture ~1,800 points per line, but standard processing keeps only
32. Storing and using the full profile enables whole-surface evenness analysis and spotting construction defects —
at almost no additional survey cost.

## The result: a road digital twin

Put together, these upgrades turn a periodic condition report into a **living digital model** of the road: geometry,
condition, assets and imagery in one georeferenced dataset that design, maintenance, safety and finance teams can all
use.
