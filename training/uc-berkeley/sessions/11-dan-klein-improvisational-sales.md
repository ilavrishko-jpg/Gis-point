# Session Summary: Dan Klein
## Improvisational Sales, Failure, "Yes And," and Showing Up Differently When It Matters

> Source: `2026-05-14-Dan Klein.pdf` — Berkeley AgriTech Accelerator (Stanford GSB / d.school)

---

## Core Message
An embodied lesson in improvisation, failure, collaboration, and presence — training for founders who will face investors, customers, and partners when things don't go to plan. **Mistakes are not the end of the performance — they are often the moment the real performance begins.** When a founder pitches, tech fails, a slide won't load, an investor interrupts. The founder's *reaction* matters more than the perfect script.

## Key Theses
- **Failure creates attention.** When something goes wrong, people notice — it can be embarrassing or memorable; the difference is your response. Exercises (counting to three, clap/snap/stomp) show people instinctively freeze, flinch, or punish themselves. We punish ourselves defensively ("you don't have to punish me — I'm already doing it"), which is protective but not resilient.
- **Reprogram the instinct:** on a mistake, instead of flinching, **celebrate — "Ta-da!"** (a practice tool, not literal real-life behavior). Builds a wider range of responses.
- **Not all failures are equal** (Amy Edmondson, *The Right Kind of Wrong*): **intelligent failures** (experiments that generate learning) · **basic failures** (should be prevented) · **complex failures** (systemic, often emerge when small failures are hidden — e.g. Boeing). Goal: surface mistakes early enough to be useful, not celebrate incompetence.
- **Cheerful shared failure builds trust.** Teams bond through success but bond *faster* failing together without blame. "Something went wrong — now let's build from it" is a leadership asset.
- **Mistakes as creative gifts:** Post-it Notes (failed strong adhesive), penicillin (unwashed petri dishes), sildenafil (failed hypertension trial). When something unexpected happens, ask: **"What is possible now?"**
- **Blocking vs. accepting:** "yes but" blocks (even a partial block is a block); **"yes and"** lets ideas grow. In sales/customer discovery, don't push a pre-written pitch — **co-create** the conversation. If a customer says "this would only work if…", explore, don't defend. Co-creating a solution with the customer builds a relationship — *that's gold.*
- **Psychological safety & hierarchy** — the submarine story: a commander dove with a hatch open; nobody spoke up. *"Today is the day you preferred to die rather than tell me I was wrong."* Visible anger makes people hide future mistakes → complex failures. (Relevant to Ukrainian culture where people grew up expecting punishment.)
- **Improvisational sales ≠ making it up or being funny** ("don't try to be funny"). It's being **dynamically responsive**: the plan may not survive the room; prepare for what goes wrong (what if the slides/projector/internet fail — could you pitch without them?); interact with the audience (ask, imagine, hold a sample, discuss with a neighbor).
- **Leadership on mistakes:** define blameworthy vs. praiseworthy; model it (don't punish yourself while telling employees to take risks); use the word **"experiments"** with a defined budget/scope; celebrate surfacing failures (Google X rewarded canceling projects that weren't working). Repetitive basic failures still need fixing (training/process/tools/roles).

*Closing (Keith Johnstone): "People who say yes are rewarded by the adventures they have. People who say no are rewarded by the safety they attain."*
