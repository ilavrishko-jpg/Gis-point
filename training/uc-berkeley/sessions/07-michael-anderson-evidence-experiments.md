# Session Summary: Michael Anderson
## Evidence as Strategy: Designing Pilots That Survive Investor and Customer Due Diligence

> Source: `2026-05-14-Michael Anderson.pdf` — Berkeley AgriTech Accelerator

---

## Core Message
Your next pilot is not just a demo — it is an opportunity to **create evidence that can travel**. A well-designed pilot wins not one customer but helps win the next ten customers, investors, partners, and regulators. For foreign founders without local credibility shortcuts, rigorous evidence compresses the credibility gap.

## Key Theses
- **Company A vs. Company B.** A: testimonials, happy-farmer photos, "users see 30% higher yields" (customers vs. non-customers). B: embedded rigorous evaluation, randomized trials, published results with confidence intervals. Five years later B is the trusted, scaled company. *(Loosely based on One Acre Fund.)* The difference is the **evidence regime**, not the product.
- **The one question every sophisticated buyer asks: "Compared to what?"** "30% higher yields" invites the counter-question — were those farms already better managed/capitalized? That's **selection bias**.
- **Three weak comparisons:** (1) customers vs. non-customers → selection bias; (2) before vs. after → confounded by weather, management, **regression to the mean**; (3) matching on observables → better, but can't adjust for invisibles (operator quality, discipline, ambition).
- **The fix: Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT).** Flip a coin per unit — the coin doesn't know which farms are better. Treatment and control end up similar on measured *and* unmeasured dimensions; the product becomes the only systematic difference. "8-14% in a randomized comparison" is smaller but far more credible than "30%." *(Method traces to R.A. Fisher, 1920s agricultural experiments.)*
- **Six design decisions:** (1) **Unit of randomization** — smallest independent unit without spillovers (cow/herd/barn/plot/field/farm/greenhouse); (2) **Sample size / power** — single digits never enough; often dozens (e.g. 60-100 herds); (3) **Pre-specify the primary outcome** — avoid the "garden of forking paths" / cherry-picking (use AsPredicted.org or the AEA RCT Registry); (4) **Spillovers & attrition** — track everyone randomized (intention-to-treat); (5) **Standardized measurement + blinding** where feasible; (6) **Time horizon matched to biology** (fertility cycles, growing seasons, ~30 days for energy proxies).
- **Scaling is a near-free experiment.** You roll out to customers over time anyway — **randomize the rollout order** among similar customers, and the later cohort becomes the control group at near-zero marginal cost.
- **When RCTs don't fit:** single-customer bespoke deployments; non-randomizable attributes (e.g. coastal vs. inland); genuine ethical constraints (but many "ethical" objections are capacity constraints in disguise → randomizing the rationing is fairer). Alternatives: difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity, synthetic controls.
- **Playbook:** reframe the next pilot as an experiment → pre-register the design → partner with academics (they want a publication; low cost) → plan the boring details → be skeptical of your own flattering pilot data → treat experimentation as a **go-to-market cost**, not academic overhead.

*A demo wins one customer. A well-designed experiment wins a category.*
