# Session Summary: Gauthier Vasseur (Session 2)
## AI Demystified: Business Intelligence, Machine Learning Families, Natural Language Coding, and Markdown

> Source: `2026-05-13-Gauthier Vasseur.pdf` — Berkeley AgriTech Accelerator

---

## Core Message
Don't let vendors, buzzwords, or hype confuse you. **AI is not magic and not one thing.** "We do AI" is too vague to mean anything. Founders must distinguish **artificial intelligence vs. machine learning vs. deep learning vs. generative AI** to make better technology decisions and know when AI is actually the right tool. The point isn't to sound sophisticated — it's to solve business problems faster.

## Key Theses
- **AI starts with the business question.** Not every question needs AI — sometimes the right answer is clean data, business intelligence, a better workflow, or a simple chart. Putting AI everywhere is confusion, not strategy.
- **80% of AI work is data.** If data isn't clean, structured, relevant, and connected, AI fails or produces unreliable output. Think data + systems + people + processes together.
- **Machine learning is not mysterious — it mostly does four things:**
  - **Regression** → predict a number (yield prediction, demand forecasting)
  - **Classification** → predict a category / yes-no (cow health alerts, disease risk, equipment failure)
  - **Clustering** → group by similarity (field grouping, customer segmentation)
  - **Time series prediction** → extrapolate the future from past time-based data
- **ML vs. generative AI:** ML learns patterns from past data to predict on new data; generative AI creates content. Prompting ChatGPT ≠ building an ML model.
- **The hard part is not the algorithm** — it's the data, the question, and the **domain expertise**. This is exactly where these founders have an advantage: they know the fields, cows, farmers, machines, climate, and operational reality. AI is powerful only when that expertise guides it.
- **Three practical productivity systems** (continued from the data session): AI literacy, business intelligence (e.g. Pyramid Analytics for large files via joins/light transforms — reduces Excel dependence), **natural language coding**, and **Markdown-based content production**.
- **Markdown as a productivity system:** simple, durable, transparent, machine-readable — the natural interface between humans and LLMs.

*The real question is never "how do we use AI?" but "what business problem are we solving, and what is the right method?"*
